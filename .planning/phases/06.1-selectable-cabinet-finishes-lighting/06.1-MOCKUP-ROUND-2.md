## 2. The two contract changes you must confirm

### S-1. Paint the light falloff on the page instead of a fixed layer (C17)

Sheet: `sheets/round2-falloff.png` (walnut on warm stone and oak; the first round's fixed layer against the same gradient painted on the page; top of the page and scrolled 650 px).

- **Yes (recommended).** `body` paints the falloff as a top-anchored background of `100lvh`, so it scrolls with the page, like a light from the ceiling that stays at the top. The fixed full-viewport layer disappears: one layer fewer on phones, and no grey text anti-aliasing from a fixed layer (the first round measured text edges changing by up to 115 of 255 with it, finding F1, and had to hide the layer on warm brown to protect the frozen default). This changes the FIXED "`html::before`, `100lvh`" line of the contract.
- **No.** Keep the fixed layer for the non-brown woods; warm brown has none. Text on the other four walls keeps the grey anti-aliasing the first round found.
- **What to look at:** the two look the same at the top of the page. They differ only when scrolled: on the page the glow scrolls away, with the fixed layer it stays on screen. On the long phone page (the 400 sample is about 15,000 px tall) the painted version leaves the lower part of the page as plain wall. I judge that fine, but it is a taste call as well as a contract one.

### S-2. Deep: merge the floor into the inner-wall element, or keep six layers (C13 and C11)

Sheet: `sheets/round2-depth.png` (Deep on Classic + warm brown and On legs + oak, merged against mitred; Deep + lights on mahogany).

- **Merged (recommended).** The inner walls, ceiling and floor are four gradient layers on the one decoration element: ceiling, left wall, right wall, floor. No clip-path and no layer on the cubby itself. The two mitred corners are gone, because they were what made the first round's Deep read as a picture frame. Within the contract's "at most five layers".
- **Six layers.** The floor stays a layer of the cubby, and the wall element keeps its two mitred corners (five layers and a clip-path): six layers per cubby, one over the contract's "at most five" budget. It is also the more expensive one on a phone.
- **Cost on a phone** (the 400 sample, phone layout, Deep and lights Always on, trace under 6x CPU throttle, median of three runs; headless on this machine, so read the ratios and not the milliseconds). Only rendered layers are counted: an element under `display: none`, such as the light layer while the lights are Off, is not painted and is not counted (the first count of this table included 352 such layers):

**Where this round differs from the Critic's text, and why**

1. **Pool width.** The Critic's `ellipse 75% 50%` with its stops still draws a bell: the stops count from the centre of the ellipse, so a top corner of the cubby sits at about 0.06 alpha against 0.55 at the centre, not "about 80 %". I used `200% 50%`, which keeps the corners at about 80 % and reads as a strip. The stops and the hairline are the Critic's.
2. **Birch pool strength 0.12** instead of 0.18 (see `R-10.2`).
3. **Floor fade ends at 96 %** of its height, not 100 %, so the bottom row of the section box matches the wall outside it.
4. **Plywood on legs:** its legs were already one plain tone, so the vertical-grain fix applies to On legs only. While doing it I found that the first round's legs recipe gave four size entries to seven background layers (the sizes repeat in a cycle), which put the grain in the wrong boxes and is part of why the legs looked striped. The build must write one size and one position per background layer.
5. **Critic C18** (bottom-aligned rows) is not drawable here; carried as written.

**Findings I leave open**

- **Birch evening is quiet, and now visible.** The hot line gets a short warm fill under it that cancels the shelf's own shadow (K3, section 7). The wash stays faint by design, because a strong pool lifts the light back into the light spine tones.
- **The light-wall logo is a labelled stand-in box** on birch and now on walnut as well, because the official dark logo comes from your pack in the build.
- **Lit floor glow at miniature size.** The skirting wash behind the legs and base that made pale stubs in the 35-pair miniatures is removed (K5, section 7); only the glow on the floor in front of the base is left.
- **Deep still costs about 58 % more rendered gradient layers on a phone** than today (merged, section 2), and a real-phone check is open (C13).
