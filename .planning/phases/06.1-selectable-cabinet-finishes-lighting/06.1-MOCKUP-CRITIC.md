# Phase 6.1 mock-up round: independent Critic pass

Second look before the owner reviews `06.1-MOCKUP-ROUND.md`. Done with the senior-frontend checklist, on the round's own sheets (`ui-refs/mockup-round/sheets/`), with zoomed crops, pixel sampling, and a few headless renders of `source/switcher.html` to confirm what the sheets show. Contrast numbers below were recomputed with the same model the contract uses (gamma-correct blend, WCAG ratio, weakest of the 12 spine tones). All content is synthetic.

## Verdict: approve with changes

The seven looks are real, distinct pieces of furniture, except for two near-twin pairs (C7). The layer split is solid: zero-pixel baseline, no geometry shift, paint stays inside the box. Approve the looks as directions.

Do not approve two things yet:

- **The focus ring sheet.** On birch the box ring is ink on ink, and on a painted back it is 1.00:1. This is a cascade bug in the mock, not a taste call (C1).
- **R-10 as asked.** Do not accept the five values under 1.3 by name. Three of them come from the strip band stacking on top of the pool (C3). One comes from painted backs inheriting birch's weak lamp (C4). The last two come from plank sheen on the wooden backs (C10). All five have token fixes. A small second round should re-measure the lamp and R-10 after those fixes. The R-10 sheet also does not show the case being decided (C2).

Severity key: **blocker** means fix before the owner answers that item. **should** means fix in this round or carry into the build as a required change. **nice** means optional polish.

## Findings

### C1. Focus ring on birch and on painted backs is ink on ink
- **Concerns:** L6, L7, `focus-rings.png`, the executor's taste call about a "thinner halo".
- **What I see:** `focus-rings.png`, birch tiles. Pixel sampling shows 10 px of solid ink and no light accent ring. The rendered page confirms it: the computed outline colour of a focused box is `#d9b98a` on brown and oak, but `#2a1a10` (ink) on birch and on birch with painted backs.
- **Why:** `site.css` is not in a layer. Its `button:focus-visible { outline: 2px solid var(--room-ring) }` beats the layered `.placement:focus-visible` rule, because a rule outside any layer always wins over a layered one. On the birch wall `--room-ring` is ink, so the box gets an ink ring inside an ink halo. On a painted back, ink against petrol `#0f2025` is **1.00:1**: where the ring meets the back, it disappears. The contract wants the accent ring (8.98:1 on petrol) plus the ink halo. The executor reported "rings visible on every wood" and offered a thinner halo. Both rest on this bug.
- **Change:** keep the generic chrome rule off boxes: `a:focus-visible, button:not(.placement):focus-visible`. Another way is to put the chrome rules of `site.css` in a layer declared before `structure`. Then re-shoot `focus-rings.png` and withdraw the halo taste call. Add a browser test that reads the computed outline colour of a focused box for every wood × backs pair (it must be `#d9b98a`), and that measures the ring at least 3:1 against the painted back.
- **Severity:** blocker for the focus ring sheet and for the build. The looks themselves are not blocked.

### C2. The R-10 sheet does not show the case being decided
- **Concerns:** R-10, `decision-R-10.png`.
- **What I see:** The last tile, painted back on birch, is cut off at the right edge. That is the tile with the weakest value (1.25). Every tile shows a Teal box. The values the owner is asked to accept are for Navy and Rose, and Teal is not the weakest tone on any back.

- **Severity:** blocker for R-10 only.

### C3. The lamp reads as beige spotlights, and the strip band pushes the peak past its sweet spot
- **Concerns:** lights on every dark back, R-10, R-8, `look-L1-evening-desktop.jpg`, `look-L4/L5-evening-desktop.jpg`, `depth-strip.png` (Deep + Always on), `decision-R-8.png`.
- **What I see:** In every cubby the pool is a bell-shaped beige cap centred under the shelf. It looks like a row of puck lights or lampshades, not an LED strip hidden behind the shelf edge. The source line (the hairline) is not visible at desktop size. The pools look like a dusty tan smear more than glowing light.
- **Why, look:** The pool is an ellipse 90 % wide with a flat cap (0.55 from 0 to 24 % of the radius) and then a straight fall. That cap has a visible edge, which draws the bell. A strip light lights the full width of the back, with only a soft fall-off at the corners.
- **Why, numbers:** At the peak, a dark back is only clear of the spine tones in a narrow window around alpha 0.55. Recomputed for brown: 0.50 gives 1.26, 0.55 gives 1.43, 0.65 gives 1.28. The round added a separate band (`0.22 × lamp-k`, top 14 %) on top of the pool. In the top of the cubby the two stack to about 0.65 (brown) or 0.71 (mahogany, `--lamp-k` 1.1). That lifts the back past the window into the light spine tones. This is why every measured peak lists **Rose** as the weakest tone, while the pool alone would leave Rust weakest. Pool plus band in the model gives: brown 1.29, walnut 1.26, oak 1.25, mahogany 1.18, painted on birch 1.20. The band is the main cause of the low peaks in the R-10 table.
- **Change** (all START values in `modifiers.css`):
  1. Drop the separate 0.22 band.
  2. Make the wash wide and soft: `radial-gradient(ellipse 75% 50% at 50% 0%, …)`, which is 150 % of the cubby wide, so the top corners keep about 80 % of the centre. Smooth the stops: `0.55k 0`, `0.55k 18%`, `0.30k 34%`, `0.12k 50%`, `0.05k 70%`, `transparent 100%`. The peak alpha then stays at exactly `0.55 × --pool-k` (see C4) wherever a box can reach.
  3. Make the source visible: hairline `max(2px, 2.5 * var(--u))` at alpha 0.8 in a hotter tint (`--lamp-hot-rgb: 255 236 205`). It sits above every box, so it costs no contrast.
  4. Re-measure R-10 after this change.
- **Severity:** should (this is the look of the "Evenings only" feature, and it decides R-10).

### C4. Painted backs inherit birch's weak lamp
- **Concerns:** L7, R-7, R-10 (painted on birch, 1.25).
- **What I see:** `look-L7-evening-desktop.jpg` against `look-L7-desktop.jpg`: the largest pixel change is 36 of 255. The evening is effectively invisible. The same holds for L6 (35 of 255).
- **Why:** `--lamp-k` is per wood. Birch has 0.18 because its own back is light. A painted back is very dark, but it still gets 0.18. That small lift moves petrol toward Navy (1.32 in the model, 1.25 measured). At full strength the same back gives 1.42 (model), the value painted backs already get on brown.
- **Change:** split the pool strength from the wood strength. Add `--pool-k` to the wood layer, the same value as today's `--lamp-k` per wood. Add one single-attribute rule in the modifiers layer: `[data-backs="painted"] { --pool-k: var(--pool-k-painted, 1); }`. It reads one choice only, so it is not a pair rule. Spill, floor glow and the Deep top face keep `--lamp-k`. Expected: painted on birch peak about 1.42, and L7 gets a visible evening.
- **Severity:** should.

### C6. The kick tone is wrong at both ends: a ghost on warm brown, a hole on birch
- **Concerns:** L2, R-9, Modern / Display / Plywood on every wood, `pair-grid-35.png` row 1.
- **What I see:** `look-L2-desktop.jpg` and `look-L2-base.png`: the Modern toe-kick on warm brown is the same tone as the wall, and partly lighter. The base reads as a faint rectangle, and the piece seems to end at its bottom shelf. In the pair grid, Modern, Display and Plywood on warm brown show a mid-brown kick, while on walnut and mahogany the kick is near-black. On birch it is black against plaster (the executor's R-9 item).
- **Why:** Brown's `--recess` is wood mixed with 22 % black, about `#4b2d18` (luminance 0.034). The wall `#3b2416` is darker (0.022). A kick set back in shade must be darker than the wall behind it. Only Modern, Display and Plywood read `--recess`, so Classic and the frozen default do not change.
- **Change:**
  - Brown `--recess` and `--recess-low`: `color-mix(in oklch, var(--wood), black 55%)`, about `#1f0f06`. This is the same rule walnut already uses. Note this is a contract edit to the FIXED brown row. It does not touch any frozen pixel.
  - Birch: yes to the R-9 option at its upper end. `--recess` `#4a4337` (5.9:1 against the frame, 6.8:1 against the plaster), and `--recess-low` `#5f574a`, so the floor's bounce light lifts the bottom.
  - Add a token test. On a dark wall, the kick's top colour must have a lower luminance than the wall. On a light wall, it must be at least 4.5:1 against the wall, and its luminance must stay at or above 0.05 (never black).
- **Severity:** should (L2 is one of the seven looks).

### C7. Two near-twin pairs remain: walnut is almost warm brown, and Display is almost Modern
- **Concerns:** L2 against L3, R-4, R-5 wood tiles, `pair-grid-35.png`.
- **What I see:** `look-L3-desktop.jpg` reads as L2 in a mauve room with a black base. The walnut wood `#593a29` against warm brown `#6b4226` is 1.18:1. The plum wall (luminance 0.021) has the same lightness as the brown wall (0.022), only a cooler hue. In `decision-R-5.png` the warm brown and walnut corner tiles differ only by a thin wall strip. In the pair grid, Modern and Display differ mainly by top thickness (28 against 44 mm).
- **Why:** The owner split design from wood to dissolve near-twins (CONTEXT, "Specific Ideas"). Two remain.
- **Change:**
  - **Walnut:** pick warm stone in R-4 (see the table). On stone, the walnut piece reads as dark, rich and clearly not brown. If the owner keeps plum, darken the wood to `--wood` `#4a3022` (1.40:1 against brown) and raise the figure (`--grain-k` 1.2 to 1.5).
  - **Display against Modern:** make the float the signature. Display `--base-inset` 36 to 90 mm, so the dark plinth is visibly narrower than the carcass. Float shadow ellipse as wide as the carcass plus 40 mm, alpha 0.8. Keep Modern a plain, grounded toe-kick (inset 28, `--kick-a` 0.45, with the C6 recess).
- **Severity:** should.

### C8. On legs: the leg grain runs across the leg, and oak shows a repeating smudge pattern
- **Concerns:** L4, On legs on every wood.
- **What I see:** `look-L4-base.png` and `look-L4-feet.png`: the tapered leg has horizontal stripes, like stacked discs. `look-L4-desktop.jpg` and `look-L4-corner.png`: dark smudges repeat at even spacing along the top, the apron and the shelf fronts. It reads as a pattern, not as grain. The evening sheet makes it worse on the bottom board.
- **Why:** The leg polygon is painted with `var(--h-face)`, the horizontal board recipe. A leg is a vertical member, and cross grain on a leg is the first thing that reads as fake. The smudges come from the `--streak` layer: `repeating-linear-gradient(to right, …)` with a 263 mm period. That draws vertical bars across horizontal boards. At oak's `--grain-k` 1.5 their alpha is 0.30.
- **Change:** paint the legs with `var(--v-face)`, on both designs with legs. On every soft member, cut `--streak` to `0.08 × --grain-k`. Or drop it: the tonal wander layer already varies the tone along the board.
- **Severity:** should.

### C9. Large faces read as ribbed panels, and the Library toe recess is a black bar
- **Concerns:** L5 plinth, L4 apron, Library on every wood.
- **What I see:** `look-L5-base.png` and `look-L5-feet.png`: the 70 mm plinth face carries about seven evenly spaced dark and light line pairs. It looks like reeding or a slatted panel. Under it, the 16 mm toe recess is near-black with a hard top edge.
- **Why:** The soft recipe's fine grain repeats every 10.7 mm with a 0.30 × k dark line. On a 20 mm shelf that is two lines and reads fine. On a 70 mm face it becomes a regular rhythm.
- **Change:** For faces taller than about 40 mm (plinth face, apron, crown fillet, Display top), use the classic's co-prime periods (7, 11 and 17 mm) at alpha `0.15 × k`. Draw the toe recess with `--recess` to `--recess-low` and the same top shade gradient as the kick, instead of near-black.
- **Severity:** should.

### C10. R-10 on the wooden backs: darken walnut and oak a step, and make the test see what the page paints
- **Concerns:** R-10 (walnut tail 1.25, oak tail 1.24), finding F8.
- **What I see:** The measured bare values sit 0.09 to 0.15 under the table. Working back from them, the rendered walnut back has a luminance of about 0.023, while its token is 0.016. The plank lift and sheen lines add the difference.
- **Change:** walnut `--back` `#301e15` to `#261811`, and oak `#2b2117` to `#221a12`. Both stay in the very-dark safe zone (luminance about 0.011). Token model: bare 1.71 / 1.70, peak 1.41 / 1.42, tail 1.53 / 1.53. Expected on screen, after the same lift as today: tail about 1.38 to 1.40. Also make the visibility unit test add the plank lift and sheen, and every light layer that reaches the peak, before blending. Otherwise it passes while the page is about 0.1 lower. This carries F8 into the build.
- **Severity:** should.

### C11. Deep reads as picture frames rather than a box you could reach into
- **Concerns:** depth, `depth-strip.png`.
- **What I see:** The Deep cubby has inner walls of equal width on the left, top and right, with mitred corners and similar tones. It reads like a moulded frame around a panel, or a cabinet door, more than like depth. The floor is hard to find.
- **Why:** Depth comes from unequal faces. The viewer sits slightly above, so the floor is the largest and lightest inner face, the underside of the shelf above is thin and darkest, and the side walls darken toward the back.
- **Change** (START values): floor depth 16 to 24 mm, and lighter, with the front mix going from 30 % to 20 % black on the dark woods. Ceiling 16 to 8 mm, darkest. Side walls a gradient from `--cubby-left` at the front edge to 70 % of it at the back. This stays inside the budget of one clip-path and five layers.
- **Severity:** should.

### C13. Performance: agree it needs a real phone, and add two checks

## Where I disagree with the executor's report

- "Rings visible and unclipped on every wood tested": not on birch or painted backs (C1). The "thinner halo" taste call is a bug, not a taste call.
- "The lit cabinet read as a cluster of spotlights", fixed by the band: it still reads as spotlights, and the band costs most of the R-10 peak values (C3).
- "A weaker lamp is not a clean fix": that is true. But the low values mostly come from stacked light, a borrowed lamp strength and plank sheen, not from the backs themselves (C3, C4, C10). So "accept by name" is not needed yet.
- "Modern and Display look close" as a taste call: I rate it, together with walnut against brown, as a should-fix (C7).

I agree with F1 to F11. F2 (stacking: top, sides and base share level 2 with the boxes) and F6 (a constant `--box-shade-rgb` of `20 10 4`, giving 4.51 on every wood) must go into the build exactly as written.

## Keep these

- The four-layer split with a zero-pixel baseline at 1440 and 390 px on both samples, and the geometry invariance across 7 × 90 combinations.
- The lamp under the boxes. Box faces stay identical in the evening (checked).
- The constant box shade (F6) and the measured-from-pixels honesty of the visibility table.
- Library's crown with dentils, and the darker brass on light woods.
- Plywood's ply edges in the corner crops, and L7's graphic petrol backs.
- Plywood on legs: tapered, lit-left legs with contact spots, and the wall shadow that lifts birch off the plaster.
- Paint containment: nothing escapes the section box, so there are no clipped shadows anywhere.
- Chrome contrast passes on all five walls, and the logo choice is never marginal.
