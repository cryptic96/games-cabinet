# Front-end review of the finishes decisions

The owner's senior-frontend lens, run over every UI decision taken in this discussion. Every decision is kept. Below are refinements (R) that sharpen how each decision is built, and a few ideas (I) on top. Answer by label: "accept all", or name the labels to change or drop.

## What was decided (kept as is)

| # | Decision |
|---|----------|
| K1 | The finish is two choices: a **design** (Classic, Modern, Display, On legs, Library, Plywood, Plywood on legs) and a **wood** (warm brown, walnut, oak, birch, mahogany). Every combination is allowed. This replaces "seven fixed finishes"; the seven earlier looks are combinations. |
| K2 | The wood brings the wall. **Painted backs** is an extra option for any design. |
| K3 | Today's look (Classic + warm brown, no painted backs, Shaded) is frozen; a before/after screenshot proves it. |
| K4 | **Depth** (Flat, Shaded, Deep) is its own setting and works the same on every design; Display's "seen from above" view becomes part of Deep. Default Shaded. |
| K5 | Lights are one control: **Off · Always on · Evenings only**. Evenings follow the season, sunset to sunrise from a fixed date table, by the visitor's clock, with no location. |

## Refinements

**R1. Three independent layers, so changing wood never moves anything.**
The page root carries five plain attributes: design, wood, depth, lights and backs. The CSS is three layers: the **design** layer owns every size (top, base, legs, side trim, the space each section reserves); the **wood** layer owns only colours, grain, wall, floor and the logo tone; the **modifiers** (depth, lights, painted backs, evening) add overlays. Because only the design sets heights, changing wood, lights or depth can never shift the layout or clip a leg. The same attributes work on the settings preview's own wrapper, so the preview is the real cabinet code, not a copy.

**R2. Older exports keep working.**
The stored setting grows from one word (`classic`) into design, wood, backs, lights and depth. An owner-data file or export from before this phase restores as Classic + warm brown, no painted backs, lights off, Shaded: exactly today's look. Unknown values fall back per field, not all at once, so one bad value never resets the whole look.

**R3. Contrast is checked by what can fall on a box, as a test.**
Spine labels only change contrast through what lies over them: the light and the depth shading. Wood and design never touch a box face. So one unit test runs every palette colour against every light state × depth (nine cases) and fails under 4.5:1, instead of judging 35 × 2 × 3 × 3 combinations by eye. A second test checks header, status line, footer text and the logo on each of the five walls, in day and evening, and focus rings at 3:1 on every wall and every wood frame.

**R4. Box fronts get almost no light.**
The warm light is strongest on the back panel and the box tops, and only lifts box fronts slightly. That keeps white titles on light spines readable (R3 proves the limit) and is also how a strip light behind a shelf edge really falls.

**R5. One light layer per section, faded on the compositor.**
Gradients on every cubby cannot fade smoothly: animating them repaints about 150 cubbies per frame on a phone for two seconds. Instead each section gets one extra decorative layer (hidden from screen readers) holding the light for all its cubbies, and only its opacity fades. This is one more finish-neutral hook next to top, trim and base, and the fade costs almost nothing.

**R6. The evening room is decided before the first paint.**
If the room dims in the evening (decided by sight, K8), a page drawn in daylight and then darkened would flash. A tiny script file in the page head (allowed under the strict CSP, no inline code) reads the clock and marks "evening" before anything is painted. The room is dark from the first frame, and the lights still fade on over about two seconds, which keeps the "lights come on" charm and drops the flash.

**R7. Lights come on at dusk, not at the exact sunset minute.**
People switch lamps on as it gets dark. Lights come on about 30 minutes before the table's sunset and go off at sunrise. The table holds local clock times with summer time already applied, a few points per month, interpolated. A page left open across either moment fades the same way.

**R8. "One floor per row" costs more than it looks.**
Each section clips all of its own painting at its edges (that is what keeps scrolling cheap on a phone). A floor running through the gaps between sections therefore cannot be drawn by the sections. It means either a page-level floor that knows where rows break (script work on every resize) or giving up that clipping. A floor patch per piece fits the current structure for free. The round will still show both (K8); this is the cost to weigh.

**R9. A design change moves heights, so keep the visitor's place.**
Designs reserve different heights (legs, crowns), so a live switch to another design changes section heights on open pages. The cross-fade uses the browser's view transition where available. The page keeps the section that was in view where it was, so nobody is scrolled away. Changing wood, lights or depth moves nothing (R1).

## Ideas

**I-A. Tiles that preview themselves.**
The design tiles are drawn in the wood you have chosen, and the wood tiles on the design you have chosen. The row of tiles is then already a small preview before you look at the big one.

**I-B. The lights reach the floor.**
In the evening, a faint warm glow on the floor and skirting just in front of each lit cabinet. It is one more gradient on the floor patch, and it makes the lights read as real lights in a room.
