# Front-end review of the finishes design contract

**Outcome (2026-10-13):** the owner accepted every item (U1 to U3, S1 to S8). For T1 they chose oak dark inside and birch light inside, and for T2 once per tab. All of it is folded into `06.1-UI-SPEC.md`.

The owner's senior-frontend lens, run over `06.1-UI-SPEC.md`. The contract is strong:
- the may / may-not table for the four CSS layers makes "changing the wood never moves anything" testable;
- the frozen default is proven by a zero-pixel test written first;
- putting the lamp **under** the boxes, with a fixed stacking map, keeps every spine label at 4.5:1 by construction;
- the logo switch point (luminance 0.27) is computed, not guessed;
- owner chrome keeps today's tokens, so a light wall can't flip its text.

All of that is kept. The review checked one thing the contract did not compute: whether the **boxes** stay visible against the back, with the lamp on as well as off. That gives the two biggest items below.

Answer by label: "accept all", or name the labels to change or drop. U = refinement, S = smaller item, T = taste call (pick one).

All ratios below were computed with the WCAG formula, blending in sRGB the way the browser does.

## Why boxes vanish: the palette has two bands

The 12 spine tones fall into two luminance bands:
- **dark:** Navy, Plum, Wine, Slate, Forest, Teal, Rust (0.054 to 0.149);
- **light:** Pink, Mustard, Sage, Sky, Cream (0.417 to 0.772).

A back panel inside either band matches one of the boxes. Two places are safe:
- **very dark**, at luminance 0.017 or below (like today's back): weakest box 1.57 to 1.85;
- **the gap** between the bands, around 0.25: weakest box about 1.5.

Today's weakest box against today's back is Navy at 1.66:1.

## Refinements

**U1. Lights on make the dark boxes vanish into the glow.**
The contract's lamp pool is a soft blob, 0.34 at its peak. On every dark back that peak lands right on the dark tones:

| Back | Bare back, weakest box | Lamp at 0.34 peak, weakest box |
|------|------------------------|--------------------------------|
| Warm brown | 1.66 (Navy) | **1.20** (Teal) |
| Walnut | 1.51 | **1.10** |
| Mahogany | 1.85 | **1.21** |
| Painted petrol | 1.20 | **1.01** |

The peak sits at the top of each cubby, right where the box tops are.

- **Proposal:** make the pool a **strong, short** strip-light band.
  - It peaks at about 0.55 in the top 12 % of the cubby, the clearance above the boxes, and falls to about 0.05 by 35 %.
  - Computed on the dark backs: about 1.35 to 1.48 at the peak and 1.49 to 1.69 in the tail. Only a thin ring in between crosses the dark band.
- It also looks more like a real LED strip: a bright band near the source with a quick falloff. A soft, mid-strength blob reads as a vignette rather than a lamp.
- The round's visibility check then measures box against back at the **peak and the tail** as well as on the bare back.

**U2. The new backs sit on palette tones.**

| Back | Luminance | Weakest box |
|------|-----------|-------------|
| Oak `#6a5136` | 0.092, in the dark band | 1.10 (Forest) |
| Painted petrol `#213a40` | 0.037, in the dark band | 1.20 (Navy) |
| Birch `#c6b193` | 0.456, in the light band | 1.03 (Sage) |

- The birch back is also only 1.26:1 against the birch frame (brown is 1.94, oak 3.17), so its cubbies don't read as recesses. A recess should be clearly darker than the front.
- **Proposal:** move every back out of the bands. The `--keyline` outline then becomes a fallback rather than the fix, since outlines read as drawn rather than physical.
  - **Painted backs:** deepen the petrol to `#0f2025`. That is 1.66:1, the same as today, and still reads as dark petrol paint.
  - **Oak and birch:** see T1.
  - The Deep inner walls and floor are re-derived so they sit between each wood's frame and its new back.
- With the darker backs, the "+N more" chip also improves: on birch it goes from 1.11 to 1.80.

**U3. On a phone, the settings preview is cropped and the controls are far away.**
- **The crop.**
  - The preview is sized by height alone (46 % of the screen height).
  - A three-cubby sample section is about 1.3 times wider than tall. At 390 px high it needs about 510 px of width, but a phone has about 358 px.
  - Because the wrapper is `overflow: hidden`, both sides are cut off, on the very device the owner uses to show the live switch.
  - **Fix:** size it by both limits, `width: min(100%, calc(min(46dvh, 28rem) * var(--section-ratio)))`, and let the height follow from the aspect ratio. The design layer exposes the ratio it already computes.
- **The distance.**
  - Seven design tiles and five wood tiles in two columns make about six rows of tiles, roughly 1,100 px of scrolling, between the preview and the Lights and Depth controls.
  - **Proposal:** below 40rem, Design and Wood each become one horizontal row with scroll snapping. Each tile is about 7rem wide, and the next one peeks in so the row clearly scrolls.
  - The tiles stay native radios, so arrow keys still work and the checked tile scrolls into view.
  - Lights, Depth and the buttons then sit within about one screen under the preview.
- **Desktop:** tiles use `repeat(auto-fill, minmax(7rem, 1fr))`, so the five woods share one row instead of a row of four plus one alone.

## Smaller items

**S1. Flat keeps the planks.**
The contract's Depth table says Flat has no plank seams. But D-07 makes planks part of the back's construction (the design), not of depth. Flat drops only the shading, so Classic in Flat keeps its planked back.

**S2. Preview time only works with Evenings only.**
The contract contradicts itself:
- one part says the evening marker matters only when Lights is Evenings only (which matches D-13, dimming in Evenings mode);
- another part says that with Lights off, Evening shows the dimmed room.

**Fix:** with Off or Always on, Day · Evening changes nothing. So the group is disabled (a native disabled `<fieldset>`) with the hint `Only matters with Evenings only.`, and the contradicting line goes.

**S3. One scroll keeper, not two.**
- Chrome and Firefox already keep the scroll position when content above changes size (`overflow-anchor`); Safari does not.
- The contract's own keep-the-section-in-place step could therefore fight the browser's in Chrome and Firefox.
- **Fix:** set `overflow-anchor: none` on the page while a design change is applied, so only the contract's rule moves the page.

**S4. The wall light must not jump on a phone.**
The wall's light falloff is a fixed layer the size of the screen. On a phone, the screen height changes when the address bar collapses, so the layer would resize and jump. Give it `height: 100lvh` (the largest screen height), so it never resizes and the browser only moves it.

**S5. Hairlines stay at least 1 px.**
The brass line (2 mm), the strip-light hairline and the lit edges get smaller than a pixel at phone and tile scale (about 0.25 to 0.5 px per mm) and smear. Clamp them with `max(1px, 2 * var(--u))`, the way the ply stripes already are.

**S6. The 35-pair grid shows whole miniatures, not corners.**
- The designs differ in their outline: top, base and legs. That is the contract's own argument for the design tiles (R-5). Corners can't show Library's crown in birch, or legs at all.
- **Proposal:** draw the grid as the same whole-piece miniatures as the design tiles, seven designs by five woods.
- This changes "cubby corners" in D-30, so it needs your yes.

**S7. A pale cabinet throws a shadow on a pale wall.**
- Birch on the plaster wall is 1.14:1, so the cabinet melts into the wall.
- A real cabinet stands a few centimetres in front of the wall and casts a soft shadow onto it.
- **Proposal:** add a wood-owned `--wall-shadow`: 0 on warm brown (frozen), about 0.22 on birch, about 0.15 on any other light wall. It is drawn as a soft band down the right side and under the top, inside the side allowance (at least 4 mm free on every design, 32 mm on Plywood).
- That gives the cabinet an outline without drawing one.

**S8. "Slightly lifts box fronts" becomes "doesn't lift them".**
- The contract keeps the lamp off the box fronts entirely, which is stricter than D-12's "only slightly lifts box fronts".
- It is right twice over:
  - under the default Shaded depth there is no contrast to spare (Rust is at 4.51);
  - a strip light aimed at the back panel really does leave the box fronts in the shelf's shade, so the boxes read as backlit.
- A small lift remains possible on Flat only (up to 0.08), as a round option.
- **Asked:** accept "no front lift" as the reading of D-12.

## Taste calls

**T1. The backs of oak and birch.**

| | **Airy** (light inside) | **Contrast** (dark inside) |
|---|---|---|
| Oak back | `#a3845e`, weakest box 1.51 | `#2b2117`, weakest box 1.57 |
| Birch back | `#9c8a6a`, weakest box 1.49 | the same dark tone, about 1.6 |
| Character | A pale piece with a shaded interior, closest to the earlier mock-ups | Pale frame, dark niches; the lit cubbies glow like display niches |
| Lamp | Must stay a gentle wash on these two (peak about 0.05 to 0.10). A strong pool lifts a light-inside back into the light tones (1.03) | The same strong, short pool as every other wood (U1) |

**Recommended:** Contrast for oak (oak furniture with shadowed niches, and the lamp behaves like on every other wood) and Airy for birch (birch and plywood are about lightness, and painted backs are there for contrast). Both are shown side by side in the round.

**T2. How often the lights fade on.**
- *Every page load (as decided):* the charm every time. Language switches and owner navigation also replay the 2-second fade.
- *Once per tab:* the first load in a tab fades; later loads in the same tab start lit. A session-only flag stores nothing personal and works under the strict CSP.

**Recommended:** once per tab.

## What works

- Three layers, owned by size, colour and overlay, plus "no pair selectors": this is what keeps 7 × 5 × 2 × 3 × 3 combinations sane.
- The frozen-default test written first, the computed contrast, and the cue list per design. Each starting value can be traced to a reason.
