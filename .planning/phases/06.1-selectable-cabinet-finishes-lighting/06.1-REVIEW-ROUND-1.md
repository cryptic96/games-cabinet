## The Critic pass (senior front-end lens, run on the built pages before this document)

Method: the checklist (first impression, reference fidelity, light and depth, hierarchy, colour, typography, rhythm, responsiveness, states, crops, performance smell) over every frame listed below.

Top three:

1. **Birch's evening is subtle (T1).** On birch's wood back the lamp reads as a faint darkening at the top of each cubby, not as a glow; day and evening are hard to tell apart on `L6-plywood-legs-birch-1440.png` and `L6-plywood-legs-birch-evening-1440.png`. This is the price of keeping every spine at 4.5:1 and the weakest box tone at 1.3:1 against a pale back (`--pool-k` 0.12), not a defect. Painted backs on birch (`B1-painted-birch-lit.png`) show a strong evening.
2. **"Mahogany" touches its tile's right edge on the 1440 Settings panel (T2).** The five wood tiles share one row through the tighter grid; the name, at 14 px in DejaVu, ends only a few pixels from the border (`S2-settings-tiles-1440-crop.png`). It fits, with almost no margin.
3. **Forced colours: the tile pictures disappear and the chosen tile is told apart only by its radio (F1).** Everything stays readable and usable; see F1.

Smaller items: the third phone section of the 65 sample holds only two boxes and looks sparse (a property of that sample's size, see Q1); the oak legs design shows a clear lighter floor band with a hard top edge in front of the legs (the approved floor patch, `L4-legs-oak-crop-base.png`); nothing else clipped, doubled or misaligned in the crops.

What works: the seven looks read as seven different pieces of furniture and not as recoloured copies; walls, floors and logos follow the wood; spines and box labels stay readable in every look, lit or not; Deep reads as depth; the lamp never washes over a box face; nothing moves when only the wood, backs, depth or lights change.

Changes made in the Critic pass: none. No clear defect inside the Tuning Register was found; the two findings above are taste calls for you (T1, T2).

## Values the tests forced during the build (C)

- **C1. The floor tail.** The look matrix tests found that on oak, mahogany, walnut and birch the floor patch and the floor glow left a faint tail on the last pixel row of the section box (4 to 5 of 255 from the wall, where the contract allows 3). Both now fade to nothing at 90 % of their height (were 96 % and 100 %), and the glow's strength went from 0.14 to 0.155 to keep its overall brightness. Warm brown has no floor and no glow, so the default look did not move. **Proposed: acknowledge.**
- **C2. The visibility floor table.** The accepted peak and tail values in the earlier table were measured for the earlier backs and lamp; the test now holds the values recomputed from the shipped tokens (bare / peak / tail): brown 1.66 / 1.43 / 1.49, walnut 1.71 / 1.41 / 1.53, oak 1.70 / 1.42 / 1.53, birch 1.49 / 1.39 / 1.49, mahogany 1.85 / 1.50 / 1.67, painted 1.66 / 1.42 / 1.49, each allowed to drop 0.02, none under 1.3. No back token changed because of this. **Proposed: acknowledge.**
- **C3. The chrome contrast numbers.** The measured muted-text and line ratios on the walls differ slightly from the second-round table (for example walnut muted 5.45 against 5.12, oak line 4.27 against 4.53) because the table used the mock-up's own method; every value clears its floor (text and muted 4.5, line 3), so no wall or text token changed. **Proposed: acknowledge.**

## Performance (P1, non-gating)

Method: Chromium, 390 x 844 at 3x density with touch, the 400 sample, a scripted 2-second scroll, three runs per variant; CPU throttle 4 as the plan says, and 6 for a second view. "Shipped Classic" is Classic, warm brown, wood backs, Shaded, Off.

| Classic, warm brown, Shaded, Off (shipped) | 16.0 ms | 17.0 ms | 223 ms | 316 ms |
| Classic, warm brown, Deep, Always on | 16.0 ms (ratio 1.00) | 17.0 ms | 236 ms (+6 %) | 344 ms (+9 %) |

- **P1.** The median frame time is the browser's 60 Hz cap (16 ms) for every variant, even at 6x, so frame time cannot separate the looks (as in the mock-up round); the ratio is 1.00, far inside the 25 % limit. The main-thread time per scroll separates them: the heaviest combination costs 10 % more at 4x and 15 % more at 6x, also inside 25 %. **No finding. A check on a real phone is still the only proof that Deep is smooth; it is listed for you under the release plan.** **Proposed: acknowledge.**
