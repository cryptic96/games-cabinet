# Build review round 1: independent Critic pass

Lens: senior front-end (critique checklist, materials and depth). Material: `06.1-REVIEW-ROUND-1.md`, the 114 frames in `ui-refs/build-round/`, the approved round-2 sheets in `ui-refs/mockup-round-2/sheets/`, the "Round outcome" section of `06.1-UI-SPEC.md`, both earlier critic passes, and the built CSS and JS. Every frame is synthetic. My own zoomed crops and side-by-side comparisons are in `crops/` next to this file. They are scratch files and are not for committing.

## Verdict: release with small changes

The seven looks match their approved mock-ups closely. I compared bases, corners, shelf edges and lit cubbies side by side, and measured day against evening pixel by pixel. I found no regression on the public cabinet in normal colours. The balanced, bottom-aligned sections are better than the mock-ups' uneven pairs.

The changes needed are on the owner's Settings panel and in forced colours. One of them, the BGG credit disappearing in forced colours, touches a hard rule of the project, so do it before release. None of them is a blocker.

Counts: 0 blockers, 6 should, 4 nice.

## Findings

### B1. In forced colours the "Powered by BGG" wordmark disappears

- **Concerns:** F1, M7 (the logo swap), every public page and the owner pages.
- **What I see:** `F1-forced-colors-card-1440.png` (footer, crop `crops/F1-footer.png`) and the footer of `F1-forced-colors-settings-1440.png`. Only the orange flame shows. The white wordmark sits on the white system background, and I measured pure white where the letters should be. The forced-colours frames use oak, a dark wall, so the page serves the white logo. Any dark-wall look does the same. That includes the default warm brown, so the problem already existed before this phase. This phase rewrote the logo swap, which makes now the right time to fix it.
- **Why it matters:** the project's rules ask for a legible "Powered by BGG" credit on every public page. Forced-colours visitors get a flame with no words. The link and the alt text are still there, but the credit cannot be seen.
- **Change:** draw the logo on its own plate in forced colours. Images are not recoloured, so the plate has to opt out of recolouring too. In `site.css`:
  ```css
  @media (forced-colors: active) {
    .bgg-credit img {
      forced-color-adjust: none;
      padding: 4px 6px;
      border-radius: 2px;
      background-color: #3b2416;
    }
    .bgg-credit img[src*="-dark"] {
      background-color: #fbf8f0;
    }
  }
  ```
  This works with the current `src` swap in `look-switch.js` and changes nothing in normal colours. Add a small browser check with forced colours turned on: the wordmark area must not be the same colour as the plate.
- **Severity:** should.

### B2. The design and wood tiles are half the size the owner approved, and the ply cue does not show

- **Concerns:** Q4, T2, M5, R-5 and C16 (both required by the owner), and the approved sheet `mockup-round-2/sheets/round2-tiles.png` ("Tile pictures at the 128 px picture height: ply stripes now show at tile size").
- **What I see:** `S2-settings-tiles-1440-crop.png` (zoom: `crops/S2-ply-tiles.png`). At 1440 the tile picture is about 79 x 67 CSS px. That follows from the code: the column is 496 px, five tiles with an 8 px gap make each tile about 93 px, and `aspect-ratio: 150 / 128` sets the height. On the phone the 7rem tile gives a picture of about 94 x 80 px. The contract says 128 px (112 px on a phone), and the approved sheet drew the tiles at that size. At 67 px the two Plywood tiles show plain two-tone edges, the same as Modern's arris. You cannot see ply stripes. "Plywood" and "Modern" are told apart only by the top slab. Q4 talks about the grid and leaves out this size change, so the owner would approve a smaller tile without knowing it.
- **Why it matters:** the owner chose tiles that work as small previews (R-5). The ply stripe fix (C16) was a required change, and at this size it does not show. "Mahogany" touching its tile edge (T2) is a symptom of the same squeeze.
- **Change:** put the choice to the owner in plain words:
  - (a) Accept smaller tiles beside the large preview. The preview shows the whole look anyway. Record that the ply cue shows only in the preview. Take B7 instead of T2.
  - (b) **(Recommended)** Give the Settings page more width on wide screens. From `80rem`, set `--panel-max: 76rem` on the Settings page only, so all its panels stay aligned. Inside the look panel, use `grid-template-columns: repeat(5, minmax(0, 1fr))` from 64rem, so the number of columns never depends on zoom or root font size. This gives tiles about 130 px wide and pictures about 100 px tall. The names fit at 14 to 16 px, T2 is no longer needed, and the ply edge has room to read. Between 64rem and 80rem nothing changes.

  Either way, tell the owner that the tile pictures are smaller than the approved sheet on every width, including the phone.
- **Severity:** should (the owner must decide; it is not a defect on the public page).

### B3. A disabled "Preview time" looks active and can contradict the preview

- **Concerns:** M5, the Day · Evening control.
- **What I see:** `S1-settings-1440-current.png`, `S1-settings-1440-preview-time-disabled.png`, `S1-settings-1440-always-on-preview.png` (side by side: `crops/S1-preview-time.png`), and `S1-settings-320-current.png`. When the group is disabled, the chosen pill keeps its 3 px ink ring and full-strength bold text. Only the radio dot turns grey. In `preview-time-disabled`, Lights is Off and "Evening" still shows as chosen, while the preview shows day. Elsewhere on the page, disabled things look clearly disabled: Use for everyone and Back to current get a dashed border and muted text. Also, `.look-choice:hover` has no `:not(:disabled)` guard, so a disabled pill still lights up on hover.
- **Why it matters:** the owner sees a control that looks chosen and active and that disagrees with the picture above it. The page's own disabled style is not used here.
- **Change** (`owner.css`, no layout change):
  ```css
  .look-group:disabled .look-choice {
    border-style: dashed;
    color: var(--paper-label);
    cursor: default;
  }
  .look-group:disabled .look-choice:has(input:checked) {
    background-image: none;
    box-shadow: inset 0 0 0 1px var(--paper-label);
  }
  ```
  Replace the hover rule with `.look-choice:has(input:not(:disabled)):hover`.
- **Severity:** should.

### B4. Forced colours on the Settings panel: extend F1 to the pills and the pictures

- **Concerns:** F1.
- **What I see:** `F1-forced-colors-settings-1440.png`, `F1-forced-colors-settings-390.png`. The chosen tile and the chosen Lights, Depth and Preview time pills all show their selection with an inset `box-shadow`. Forced colours drop every box shadow, so all of them fall back to the radio dot alone, not just the tiles. Each tile also keeps a large empty box where its miniature used to be, which looks broken.
- **Why it matters:** F1 as proposed fixes only the tiles. The same gap stays on six other groups.
- **Change:** take F1 and widen it:
  ```css
  @media (forced-colors: active) {
    .pic-tile:has(input:checked),
    .look-choice:has(input:checked) {
      outline: 3px solid CanvasText;
      outline-offset: -4px;
    }
    .look-swatch,
    .look-preview {
      forced-color-adjust: none;
    }
  }
  ```
  The swatches and the preview are already `aria-hidden` pictures with a text summary next to them. Opting them out of recolouring makes them draw the way an image would, instead of leaving an empty frame. The focus outline still wins on the focused control, which is fine because focus and selection sit on the same item.
- **Severity:** should (it is the F1 change, extended).

### B5. Forced colours on the public cabinet: the carcass and the box edges drop out

- **Concerns:** F1.
- **What I see:** `F1-forced-colors-cabinet-1440.png`, `F1-forced-colors-cabinet-390.png` (`crops/F1-390-strip.png`). Shelves and uprights draw as black bars. The top board, the side boards and the base do not draw at all, and the boxes in one cubby have no edges. The cabinet reads as an open table of labels. The text stays readable, which is the part that matters most.
- **Why it matters:** forced-colours visitors lose the outer shape of the piece and the boundaries of each tappable box.
- **Change** (forced colours only, so no geometry or frozen pixel moves):
  ```css
  @media (forced-colors: active) {
    .placement { outline: 1px solid CanvasText; outline-offset: -1px; }
    .section-trim { outline: 2px solid CanvasText; outline-offset: -2px; }
  }
  ```
  If another element turns out to be a better carrier for the carcass edge than `.section-trim`, the executor should use it.
- **Severity:** nice (this most likely already happened before this phase, and the text is readable).

### B6. On a phone, the last section can show two empty shelves above its only boxes

- **Concerns:** Q1.
- **What I see:** `L4-legs-oak-390.png`, `L1-classic-warm-brown-390.png`, `L3-display-walnut-390.png` (strips `crops/*-390-strip.png`). The third phone section holds two boxes on its bottom shelf, with two fully empty shelves (five empty cubbies) above them. It looks the same in every look, including the frozen Classic. "Sparse" understates it: at a glance it looks like a page that has not finished loading.
- **Why it matters:** this phase did not cause it. Fixing it now would break the zero-pixel default and the layout tests. But a visitor whose collection hits this case on a phone will notice.
- **Change:** agree with Q1 (a) for this release. Record a follow-up: in the last section of a phone layout, drop wholly empty shelves (a shorter piece) or fill from the top, so empty cubbies never sit above the only boxes.
- **Severity:** should, as a follow-up (not for this release).

### B7. T2: shrink the description and the padding, not the name

- **Concerns:** T2.
- **What I see:** `S2-settings-tiles-1440-crop.png`. Beside the preview the name is 14 px / 600 and the description is 14 px / 400. Setting the name to 13 px, as T2 proposes, makes the name smaller than its own description, which turns the hierarchy upside down. CI draws with DejaVu, which is wider than most real system fonts, so on real machines the squeeze is milder than on the frame.
- **Change** (only if B2 (a) is chosen; B2 (b) makes this moot): from 64rem keep `.look-tile .pic-label` at 14 px. Set `.look-tile .pic-sub { font-size: 13px; }` and `.look-tile { padding: 6px 4px; }`. That gives "Mahogany" about 6 px of room and keeps the name above the description.
- **Severity:** nice.

### B8. T1 birch evening: keep it, but fix the description and offer one safe extra

- **Concerns:** T1, L6.
- **What I see:** `L6-plywood-legs-birch-1440.png` against `-evening-1440.png` (crop `crops/L6-day-vs-eve.png`). The average change from day to evening is 1.3 of 255 on birch, against 7.3 to 8.5 on the other woods, so the evening is about six times weaker. The document says it reads as "a faint darkening at the top of each cubby". It is the opposite: the warm fill lifts away the day's top shade, and a pale hot line appears under the shelf.
- **Change:** keep (a). Correct the wording in the round document so the owner knows what to look for. Optional taste call: on birch's wood back only, warm the hot line, for example `--lamp-hot-rgb: 255 206 140`, reset by `[data-backs="painted"]`. The line sits in the top 2.5 mm of the cubby, above the box tops and far from the 6 % and 35 % sample points, so the visibility floor does not move.
- **Severity:** nice.

### B9. The round does not show the focus rings the owner made a required change

- **Concerns:** C1 (the owner required the box focus ring to be the accent with an ink halo on birch and on painted backs), M5.
- **What I see:** none of the 114 frames shows a focused box or a focused tile or pill. Browser tests cover the computed outline colour, but the owner has no picture showing it landed. The mock-up round had `round2-focus-rings.png`.
- **Change:** add one sheet to the round: a focused box on each of the five woods and on the painted back, at 1440 and 390, plus a focused tile and a focused pill on the Settings panel. This needs no code change.
- **Severity:** should (round material only).

### B10. Settings: small hierarchy and wrapping touches

- **Concerns:** M5.
- **What I see:** `S1-settings-1440-trying-display-walnut.png`, `S1-settings-390-trying-display-walnut.png`, `S1-settings-320-current.png`. "Trying: …" has the same weight and colour as "Visitors see: …", yet it is the line that matters while the owner experiments. The summary wraps with a "·" at the start of a line ("· Lights: Off · Depth: Shaded" at 390 and 320).
- **Change:** `.look-line-trying { color: var(--ink); font-weight: 600; }`. In the summary, put a no-break space before each separator (`" · "`) so the dot stays at the end of the previous item.
- **Severity:** nice.

## What is good and should be kept

- The seven looks read as seven different pieces of furniture, and each matches its approved sheet: bases, corners, shelf edges and lit cubbies line up side by side with the round-2 crops.
- Balanced sections that share one feet line at 1440. They are better than the mock-ups' uneven pairs.
- The lamp as a strip with a hot source line. The painted back gives a clear evening. The keyline appears only while lit.
- Walls, floors and logos follow the wood. The dark BGG logo on the stone and birch walls is crisp.
- The Settings preview runs the real renderer. The tiles are drawn in the trial look. The "Visitors see" and "Trying" lines, the dashed disabled publish buttons and Undo all make sense. On the phone, the sideways tile rows with a peeking next tile work well.
- The default look is identical to the release, with zero differing pixels. The live switch keeps the visitor's place.
