## Findings the owner should know (promise versus measured behaviour)

Measured on seeded 120-game collections with 15 percent expansions, 200 seeds:

- **Balancing falls back to the plain arrangement about one time in twelve.** 16 of 200 collections that need three sections stay at rows 5, 5, 2 or 5, 5, 3, because no row budget both keeps three sections and keeps every section within one row. So "every desktop section within one shelf row of every other" holds on the 12, 65 and 400 samples but not for every collection. The layout guide says so; a test asserts that the fallback is exactly the plain arrangement and rare (under a quarter of seeds). Improving the search so it also covers those cases would be an engine change.
- **Adding a game can change a second cubby inside a section without crossing a boundary** (1 case of 199 non-series cases): raising the last section's row budget let a flat-lying box stand again. The owner's sentence says "at most one cubby inside a section"; the guide keeps the sentence and adds this caveat. Crossings of a section boundary: 10 of 199 (largest 37 games). Everything else changes one cubby.
- Poses can differ between an arrangement before and after an added game (a flat box standing again, or the reverse) when the row budget changes; the pose-stability test is therefore pinned on the section-by-section arrangement.
