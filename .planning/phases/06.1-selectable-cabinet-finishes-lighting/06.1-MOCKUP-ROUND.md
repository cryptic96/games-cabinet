# Phase 6.1: the mock-up round

Nothing of the look has been built in the repository. Everything below was drawn in a scratch copy by the real cabinet code (the renderer's own markup for the invented 65 and 400 samples) with the stylesheets ported into the four layers. Every picture is synthetic: invented titles, no real collection, no names, no places.

**How to answer.** Reply "approve all", or name labels to change, for example "L4 legs radius 8, R-2 room dims, R-4 warm stone". Silence on an item means the recommended default, except **R-10**, where any value below its table value (and the five measured values under 1.3 listed there) needs an explicit per-back yes.

Everything is relative to this folder: `ui-refs/mockup-round/`.

## 5. Round decisions R-1 to R-10

Each decision is one sheet, `sheets/decision-R-N.png`, with every option drawn by the real layers (token overrides were applied on the page only). The recommended default is the UI-SPEC's. Nothing here is decided for you.

**R-1. Phone: every section a full piece, or sections joined (per design).** Sheet: Library + mahogany and On legs + oak, separate against joined, on the phone layout.
- *Separate (recommended default, all seven designs):* free, no new hook, safe for Classic. Reads as several small cabinets stacked, most noticeable with legs and a crown.
- *Joined:* top only on the first section, base and legs only on the last. Needs a position hook and a per-position reserved height. The sheet shows the cost the contract predicted: at every join there are two boards where a real piece has one (the lower section's top rail plus the upper section's bottom shelf), so the join must be designed. Classic joined would be an explicit exception to the frozen default.

**R-2. Room dimming in the evening.** Sheet: oak, mahogany and birch walls in the evening, lights only against room dims.
- *Lights only (recommended default):* no dusk layer, no light-wall limit, one test dimension fewer.
- *Room dims:* wall 0.35 and furniture 0.25 on dark walls, wall 0.08 on the light wall. On the dark walls the dim is subtle next to what the lamps already do. **How the mock draws the furniture dim:** the furniture colours are mixed toward the dusk colour (a token mix), because a box-sized dusk layer in the light layer would also tint the wall inside the section's box and show as a rectangle (finding F7). The planned wall dim and the light-wall limit are as written. On birch the furniture dim 0.25 made the pale frame look muddy, so the mock uses 0.12 for birch (finding F9).

**R-3. Floor.** Sheet: On legs + oak, a patch per piece against no floor against one floor per row (a mock).
- *Patch per piece (recommended default):* skirting and floor inside each section, fading before the box edge; fits paint containment for free (the ring check below found nothing outside any box).
- *One floor per row:* the sheet shows the real cost. The two pieces in a row have different heights (the second section is shorter), so one band can match only one feet line and the other piece floats above it. Making it right needs script that knows where rows break, on every resize, or giving up the paint clipping on the section.

**R-4. Walnut's wall.** Sheet: Display + walnut on deep plum `#2f2531`, charcoal moss `#2e342b`, warm stone `#d2ccc0` (dark text, dark logo stand-in, wall shadow 0.15).
- *Deep plum (recommended default):* dark, white logo. Frame against wall 1.4 to 1; it stands off the wall by its lit arris and the cast shadow, which the sheet shows working.
- *Charcoal moss:* dark; close to mahogany's green.
- *Warm stone:* light, a second light wall next to birch; the strongest silhouette for the dark frame, the weakest evening. Computed: text 10.3, muted 4.8, line 3.7.

**R-5. Design tile picture.** Sheet: all seven designs as whole miniatures against corner crops, and the five woods the same way, at the 128 px picture height.
- *Whole miniature for design tiles, corner for wood tiles (recommended default):* the silhouette is the design (legs, crown, base are invisible in a corner).
- *Corner crop for both:* the sheet shows the one thing a corner shows better: the ply stripes of Plywood read strongly in the corner crop and almost vanish in the whole miniature at this size (the period is clamped to 3 px).

**R-6. Warm brown's room.** Sheet: Classic, Library and On legs on warm brown, identity against floor, skirting and falloff.
- *Identity (recommended default, frozen):* flat wall, no floor, no skirting, no falloff.
- *Alternative:* a floor with skirting and a soft falloff; an explicit exception to the frozen default. The difference is quiet on Classic and Library and clearest between the legs of On legs.

**R-7. Painted back colour.** Sheet: the five woods with one petrol `#0f2025`, then with a dark tuned per wood (`#161f15`, `#1c1520`, `#0e1d2b`, `#0f2025`, `#0b1f16`).
- *One petrol (recommended default):* one colour, weakest box 1.66 to 1 against a bare back.
- *Per wood:* five colours to review and test; all five tuned values are inside the very-dark safe zone (luminance 0.009 to 0.013, limit 0.017). Costs five more rows in the contrast test.

**R-8. Lamp colour.** Sheet: Library + mahogany and On legs + oak at 2700 K (`255 200 130`), 3300 K (`255 212 150`) and 4000 K (`255 228 190`).
- Recommended default: 3300 K. 2700 K is the warmest, candle-like; 4000 K reads grey-white on the oak.

**R-9. Odd pairs.** All 35 are in `pair-grid-35.png`. No pair rule was written. Three token fixes were made and a fourth is offered (`decision-R-9.png`):
- Library on the light woods: the brass `#c4a15a` was the same gold as oak and birch and vanished. Fixed by a darker brass token on oak (`#7d5a1e`) and birch (`#876528`). Done.
- Plywood on dark woods: the derived ply tones were too close, so the layers merged. Fixed by wider tone steps and a darker glue line in the base wood block (white 16 %, black 14 %, glue black 60 %). Done.
- Dim dusk on birch furniture: 0.12 instead of 0.25 (R-2, finding F9). Done.
- **Needs your word:** the near-black kick on birch (Modern, Display and Plywood) reads as a hole against the plaster. The sheet offers `#3a3329` to `#4a4337`. It is the table value today; changing it lightens all three designs on birch.
- Taste calls with no change proposed: Modern and Display differ mainly in the top and the kick and look close in a thumbnail; Classic in birch has a black arch against a pale base.

**R-10. Box visibility on the new backs.** Sheet: every back bare and under the lamp. Measured **from the rendered pixels** (an empty cubby, lamp pool on, weakest of the 12 spine tones against the sampled back colour), with the UI-SPEC table next to it:

| Back | Table bare / peak / tail | Measured bare | Measured peak | Measured tail | Peak and tail at 0.8 lamp |
|------|--------------------------|---------------|---------------|---------------|---------------------------|
| Warm brown | 1.66 / 1.43 / 1.49 | 1.51 (Navy) | 1.37 (Rose) | 1.32 (Navy) | 1.31 / 1.36 |
| Walnut `#301e15` | 1.58 / 1.48 / 1.40 | 1.42 (Navy) | 1.35 (Rose) | **1.25** (Navy) | 1.35 / 1.28 |
| Oak `#2b2117` | 1.57 / 1.48 / 1.39 | 1.41 (Navy) | 1.34 (Rose) | **1.24** (Navy) | 1.35 / 1.27 |
| Birch `#9c8a6a` | 1.49 / 1.35 / 1.47 | 1.42 (Rose) | **1.27** (Rose) | 1.41 (Rose) | 1.30 / 1.41 |
| Mahogany | 1.85 / 1.50 / 1.67 | 1.71 (Navy) | **1.26** (Rose) | 1.50 (Navy) | 1.38 / 1.55 |
| Painted `#0f2025` on brown | 1.66 / 1.42 / 1.49 | 1.66 (Navy) | 1.43 (Rose) | 1.48 (Navy) | 1.24 / 1.52 |
| Painted on birch | 1.66 / 1.32 / 1.49 | 1.66 (Navy) | **1.25** (Navy) | 1.63 (Navy) | 1.33 / 1.64 |

How to read it:
- The painted back matches the table exactly when bare (1.66), which validates the method. The wooden backs measure 0.09 to 0.15 **below** the table because the real back also carries plank sheen lines that lift it slightly; the table ignores them.
- **Five measured values are under 1.3 and so need your explicit per-back yes, or a change** (nothing is accepted by silence): walnut tail 1.25, oak tail 1.24, birch peak 1.27, mahogany peak 1.26, painted back on birch peak 1.25.
- A weaker lamp is not a clean fix: the weakest tone flips from Navy (a dark box, helped by a bright back) to Rose (a light box, hurt by a bright back), so about 1.3 to 1.4 is the most the lamp can offer at either end. At 0.8 of the strength, painted on brown drops to 1.24 at the peak.
- Boxes stay legible by their lit inset edge and the contact shadow. The outer `--keyline` (starts at 0) is the fallback for a back you do not accept.
- Recommended default: keep the table backs and lamp and accept the five named values by name, or name the ones to change.

## 6. Critic report

**Checked** (senior-frontend checklist: first impression, reference fidelity against the cue list, light and depth, hierarchy, measured colour, detail crops, performance smell), with screenshots at 1440 and 390 under the DejaVu font setup, plus structure checks on the real layers:

| Check | Result |
|-------|--------|
| Default look against the release | 0 differing pixels, 4 cases (65 and 400 samples, 1440 and 390 px) |
| Section boxes across wood, backs, depth and lights | 7 designs x 90 combinations: 0 boxes changed |
| Paint containment (the pixel ring at the left, right and bottom box edge against the wall just outside) | 35 pairs, lights on, Deep: largest difference 2 of 255 (after one fix). The top edge is not tested: a top board legitimately starts at the box top, so the planned "ring is the wall" test must exclude it |
| Horizontal overflow, console errors | None, 28 page states at 1440 and 390 |
| Spine contrast under the shade | See below |
| Chrome contrast on every wall, day and evening | See below |
| Cost of Deep with lights on | See below |
| Hover, focus rings | Rings visible and unclipped on every wood tested (`focus-rings.png`) |
| Not run | WebKit and Firefox; reduced motion (the lamp transition is written, not exercised); a real phone |

**Top three issues fixed during the round**

1. *The lit cabinet read as a cluster of spotlights.* Fix: a faint band across the top of each cubby (alpha 0.22, fading by 14 %) under the strong pool, so it reads as a strip light, not a pendant lamp.
2. *The floor patch ended in hard rectangular nubs in the evening.* Fix: the skirting wash now fades at both ends like the skirting itself, and the floor gradient ends before the box edge.
3. *Ply layers merged on dark woods and the brass vanished on light woods.* Fixed by the token changes in R-9.

**Changes within the START ranges** (all token or START values): strip-light band added to the pool; skirting and floor edge lines softened (shade 0.16, highlight 0.12) with a longer fade; floor gradient ends at 92 %; Modern kick shade 0.45 and shadow 0.50; oak and birch brass darker; ply tone steps wider; birch furniture dusk 0.12.

**Findings that change the contract (please carry into the build)**

- **F1. The fixed falloff layer costs sharp text.** A fixed layer behind the page makes Chrome draw all text with grey anti-aliasing instead of sub-pixel; on the identical markup it changed text edges by up to 115 of 255 and broke the frozen default. Fix in the mock: the layer exists only when the wood or the dusk needs it (`content` comes from a wood token, `none` on warm brown). Non-brown walls still carry it. Alternative if you care about the sharper text on desktop: paint the falloff on the body (it then scrolls with the page).
- **F2. The planned stacking map changes today's paint order.** Putting boxes at level 2 lets a box's shadow paint over the side boards (3,071 pixels, up to 12 of 255, on the default look). Fix: the top, side boards and base share level 2 with the boxes and stay above them by tree order; only the back, shelves and uprights are lit.
- **F3. Mixing colours for the dusk, even at 0 %, changes rounding** (22 pixels by 1). The dusk mix is applied only in the evening rule.
- **F4. Classic writes ten literal tints, not five.** Also the grain tint `40 20 8`, the arch tints `8 4 2` and `30 16 8`, the black and white of the sweeps, and the white and black of the board tones. The split needs tokens for each; the mock adds `--arch-top-rgb`, `--arch-bottom-rgb`, `--neutral-dark-rgb`, `--neutral-light-rgb` and tone tokens for the "white 4 %" style mixes.
- **F5. Token name clash.** `--wood-edge` and `--ink` are declared unlayered on the root in `site.css`, so a layered wood value on the root element would lose. The mock names the lit edge `--wood-lit`.
- **F6. Wood does touch a box face through the shade.** The shade over boxes read `--shade-rgb`, which differs per wood. With birch's `70 45 20` Rust drops to 4.33 and with oak's `40 22 6` to 4.44 at the shipped 0.20, both under 4.5. Fix: a separate constant `--box-shade-rgb` (`20 10 4`, the palette's shade) for the over-box layer. The contrast test must read that token.
- **F7. A dusk layer sized to the section box tints the wall inside the box.** Replaced by the token mix (see R-2).
- **F8. The visibility table is optimistic** by 0.09 to 0.15 on wooden backs (see R-10).
- **F9. Furniture dusk on a light wood at 0.25 looks muddy;** 0.12 on birch.
- **F10. Legs are measured from the carcass edge** (leg foot 40 mm in from the carcass), not from the section box; the two readings of the contract differ by the 32 mm allowance.
- **F11. Deep needs one extra node per cubby** (`cubby-deco`) for the inner walls: one clip-path and five gradient layers on it, plus one floor layer in the cubby's background. The mock also uses a floor hook per section and the light hook per section and per cubby.

**Measured spine contrast** (12 palette tones, lowest case): flat 5.28 on every wood; Deep (0.12) 4.72 to 4.87; Shaded (0.20) 4.33 (birch tint) to 4.55 with the per-wood shade tint, and **4.51 on every wood with the constant `--box-shade-rgb`** (F6). The lamp front stays 0, so the light never changes a box face.

Frame time under 4x CPU throttling, scrolling the phone page for 2 seconds: every state measured the 60 Hz cap (16.7 ms median, 16.8 ms at the 90th percentile), so this headless run **cannot separate the looks** and is inconclusive. The gradient count is the better smell: Deep with lights on adds about half again as many gradient layers to a phone page. A real-phone check is needed before the build is called smooth.

**What works** (keep): the lamp under the boxes (boxes keep their true colours in the evening), the strip light plus short pool, the per-design silhouettes in the 35-pair grid, the wall shadow lifting birch off the plaster, the ply stripes in the corner crops, the token-only wood layer (any wood on any design with no pair rule).

**Taste calls for you**
- The pool is an ellipse 90 % wide that tapers down; with the new band it reads as a strip light, but a flatter, wider pool is a pure token change if you want it.
- The ink halo on a focused box on birch reads as a thick dark block; a thinner halo is possible (the contrast only needs one of the two rings).
- Modern and Display look close at thumbnail size.
