# Phase 6.1 mock-up round 2: independent Critic pass

This is a second look at `06.1-MOCKUP-ROUND-2.md` before the owner reviews it. I used the senior-frontend checklist on the round's own sheets (`ui-refs/mockup-round-2/sheets/`), with zoomed crops and pixel diffs. I also rendered `source/switcher.html` and `source/switcher-400.html` headless, under the same DejaVu font setup, to check the numbers. All content is synthetic.

How I measured box visibility where it matters: I hid the boxes, sampled the back 3 px above each box's top edge, and compared it with that box's own colour after the over-box shade. That is the box-top edge a visitor actually sees. Contrast is the WCAG ratio, as in the round. The scripts live in scratch only.

## Verdict: approve with changes

Round 2 did its job. The lamp now reads as a hidden LED strip. The focus ring is right on every pair. Walnut no longer looks like warm brown, and Display no longer looks like Modern. The kicks on brown and birch are fixed. No blocker touches the looks.

Do not accept two things by silence:

- **R-10.2.** The table was measured at **Flat** depth, but the public default is **Shaded**. In Shaded, the top of every birch cubby measures **1.01:1** (Teal), by day as well as in the evening. The dark-back peaks drop from about 1.50 to 1.32–1.36. Under the owner's own R-10 rule, birch now needs a fix or a named yes (K1).
- **The evening ring.** The wide wash puts more box tops at luminance parity with the back than round 1 did: on the 400 sample, 55 of 396 box tops on warm brown are under 1.3, against 39 in round 1 and 3 by day. The contract accepted a *thin* ring. This one is now a band across every cubby, so the owner should say yes to it by name, or take the keyline fix (K2).

Severity key, as in the first pass: **blocker** means fix before the owner answers that label. **should** means fix in this round, or carry it into the build as a required change. **nice** means optional.

Counts: **1 blocker** (scoped to R-10.2), **5 should**, **4 nice**.

## Findings

### K1. R-10 was measured at Flat depth, and in the default depth birch fails at the top of the cubby
- **Concerns:** R-10.2, the R-10 table in the UI-SPEC "Round outcome", L6.2, the build's visibility test.
- **What I see:** `source/tools/measure2.mjs` draws the visibility stage with `depth: 'flat'`. Flat turns off the back's top shade (`0.55 × --depth-k`) and the over-box shade. The public default is Shaded, where both are on. I re-measured the medians over all cubbies of the 65 sample (Modern, 1440 px, lights on) along the centre line:

  | Back | Flat, 6 % / 35 % | **Shaded, 6 % / 35 %** | Deep, 6 % / 35 % |
  |------|------------------|------------------------|------------------|
  | Warm brown | 1.48 / 1.40 | **1.35** / 1.40 | 1.36 / 1.40 |
  | Walnut | 1.46 / 1.43 | **1.33** / 1.43 | 1.34 / 1.43 |
  | Oak | 1.47 / 1.43 | **1.36** / 1.43 | 1.38 / 1.43 |
  | Birch | 1.39 / 1.43 | **1.01 (Teal)** / 1.43 | 1.01 / 1.43 |
  | Mahogany | 1.46 / 1.56 | 1.46 / 1.56 | 1.48 / 1.56 |
  | Painted on brown | 1.42 / 1.49 | **1.32** / 1.49 | 1.33 / 1.49 |
  | Painted on birch | 1.42 / 1.49 | 1.41 / 1.49 | 1.43 / 1.49 |

  On birch, the shelf's shadow takes the pale back (bare about `#9f8d6c`) down to about `#6c5a40` at the top. That is the lightness of the Teal, Rust and Slate boxes. The 0.12 pool barely lifts it. This happens **by day too**. On the 65 sample, 8 of 65 box tops on birch in Shaded are under 1.3 with the lights off, against 0 on warm brown. On the 400 sample it is 30 of 396, with 14 under 1.15. The first round had the same thing; nobody saw it, because both rounds measured at Flat.
- **Why:** The owner's R-10 rule says anything under 1.3 needs a named yes. The document says "no back is under 1.3" and lets silence accept R-10.2. That is only true at Flat. It is also a daytime legibility gap for every visitor on birch, not only an evening one.
- **Change:**
  1. Re-measure R-10 at **Shaded** (the default) and at Deep, and put those columns in the table. Keep Flat as a reference column.
  2. Birch: add a wood-layer strength for the back's top shade, `--back-shade-k` (default 1, birch **0.35**). The design layer writes `rgb(var(--shade-deep-rgb) / calc(0.55 * var(--depth-k) * var(--back-shade-k)))`. Brown keeps 1, so no frozen pixel moves. By my model the top of a birch cubby goes to about luminance 0.20, which is about 1.45 against Teal and 1.6 against Rose. Re-measure. A pale back in a bright room has a soft shadow, so it also looks more natural.
  3. The build's visibility test must run at the default depth and add every layer that reaches the sample point (the plank lift, the sheen, the back shade, the pool and the over-box shade), as C10 and F8 already ask.
- **Severity:** **blocker for R-10.2 only** (it must not be accepted by silence). The looks are not blocked.

### K2. The wide wash moves the dark-box ring onto the box tops
- **Concerns:** P.2, R-10.2, L1.2 to L5.2 and L7.2 in the evening.
- **What I see:** Every soft light crosses the lightness of a dark box somewhere. The round says so. But the wide wash moved where that crossing falls. Along the centre line it now sits at 12–24 % of the cubby height on every dark back (1.02–1.14, table in K1). Because the wash is now 200 % wide, it does not fade out at the sides. It forms a band across the whole cubby, and that is where box tops sit: the median box top is at 31–37 % of the cubby height and the 10th percentile at 8–10 %. Box-top edges under 1.3 (under 1.15 in brackets), Modern, Shaded, lights on:

  | Back | 400 sample, day | 400, round 1 lamp | **400, round 2 lamp** | 65, round 1 lamp | 65, round 2 lamp |
  |------|-----------------|-------------------|-----------------------|------------------|------------------|
  | Warm brown | 3 (3) | 39 (21) | **55 (26)** of 396 | 7 (4) | 9 (5) of 65 |
  | Painted on brown | 3 (3) | 37 (23) | **51 (23)** | 6 (3) | 9 (5) |
  | Mahogany | 3 (1) | 33 (19) | **43 (22)** | 6 (3) | 7 (4) |
  | Painted on birch | n/a | n/a | n/a | 2 (0) | 8 (4) |

  In the L1.2 evening sheet (`look-L1-evening-desktop.jpg`), the weakest box tops are "Raxskvex Vossdle" (plum, **1.00**), "Belbarwyn" (forest, 1.10), "Morthan" (plum, 1.11) and "Orvvexford" (navy, 1.13). By day the weakest box on that page is 1.50. On screen these boxes stay readable by hue and their 1 px lit inset edge: purple against tan is clear. Their edge has no lightness step, though.
- **Why:** About one box in seven loses its top-edge lightness contrast in the evening, up from one in ten. The R-10.2 sheet (`round2-R-10.png`) shows only the best case. Its tall boxes put their tops at the peak (1.4–1.5), and the band is hidden behind them.
- **Change:** Pick one and record it, rather than silence:
  1. **Accept by name** in the owner's answer: "the evening ring on the box tops (about 14 % of boxes under 1.3 on the dark backs), readable by hue and edge". Add a box-top metric to the build test with a ceiling. For example: on the 400 sample, at most 15 % of box tops under 1.3 and at most 7 % under 1.15, per back, in Shaded.
  2. **Or use the contract's fallback** in the lit state only. Add one single-attribute rule in the modifiers layer: `[data-lit] { --keyline: var(--keyline-lit, 0.4); }`. That gives a 1 px dark outer line around each box. It is never on the face, so `--lamp-front` 0 holds. It switches without animation when `data-lit` is set, so measure that it does not pop visibly during the 2 s fade.
  
  Either way, redraw `round2-R-10.png` with a third box per back (Slate or Plum, the tones the round lists as `ringMin`) whose top sits at about 18 % of the cubby height. That is the case being decided.
- **Severity:** should, and it needs an explicit owner line, not silence.

### K3. On birch, "Evenings only" still shows almost nothing
- **Concerns:** L6.2, P.2, the settings preview's Day · Evening toggle, C5.
- **What I see:** I diffed `look-L6-desktop.jpg` against `look-L6-evening-desktop.jpg`. The only pixels that change by more than 30 sit on five rows (y ≈ 120, 300, 520, 720 and 880): the 2 px hot line under each shelf. Everything else moves by 6 of 255 or less. At 1:1 (my crop of the empty cubbies in section 2), day and evening cannot be told apart. The hot line is `rgb(234 215 173)`. It sits right under a pale birch shelf edge, so it reads as the shelf being 2 px thicker, not as a light. Meanwhile the top of the cubby stays the darkest part of the back (the shelf's shadow, about `#6c5a40`), and a light mounted under the shelf would remove exactly that. The document's "visible but quiet" is generous. The overview (`look-overview-lit.png`) shows L6 as unlit next to six lit pieces.
- **Why:** On birch, the owner can choose Evenings, press Evening in the preview, see no change, and think it is broken. C5 asked to either make it visible or tell the owner plainly.
- **Change:** Let the lamp cancel the shelf's own shadow. Do it in the existing hot-line layer, so there is no extra layer. On birch, extend that layer with a short warm fill: `linear-gradient(to bottom, rgb(var(--lamp-hot-rgb) / 0.8) 0 L, rgb(var(--lamp-rgb) / calc(0.35 * var(--fill-k))) L, transparent 14%)`. Here `L` is the line height, `--fill-k` is a new wood strength (birch 1, every other wood 0), and `--pool-k` stays 0.12. Together with K1's lighter birch shade, the top of the cubby should end up a little above the bare back, at luminance about 0.28–0.30. Rose then stays at or above 1.3. It reads as "the shadow under each shelf lights up", which is quiet but visible. Re-measure Rose at the peak. If the owner prefers no change, write in the round document: "on birch the evening changes only a hairline under each shelf".
- **Severity:** should.

### K4. Walnut's kick is near-black on the new stone wall and fails the approved C6 test
- **Concerns:** K.2, W.2, L3.2, R-4 combined with C6.
- **What I see:** `round2-kicks.png` row 2 (Modern, Display and Plywood + walnut on stone), and the walnut tile in `round2-R-10.png`. Walnut's `--recess` is `color-mix(in oklch, #593a29, black 55%)` = `#180d07`, luminance **0.005**, 12:1 against the stone. Under Modern and Plywood it reads as a solid black slab, heavier than the dark piece above it. That is the "black on plaster" look the round's own K.2 text says it avoided. R-4 moved walnut onto a light wall after C6 was written, so walnut's kick was never re-tuned.
- **Why:** The C6 token test recorded in the UI-SPEC says that on a light wall the kick's luminance must stay at or above 0.05. Walnut fails it, so the build test will fail. The rule cannot simply be met as written either: walnut's own wood is luminance 0.053, and a kick that light would match the carcass.
- **Change:**
  - Walnut `--recess` = wood mixed with 35 % black (`#2e1c12`, luminance 0.015, 10.2:1 against the stone, 1.6:1 darker than the wood). Walnut `--recess-low` = wood with 25 % black (`#3a2418`, luminance 0.022), so the bottom catches the floor's bounce light, like birch.
  - Amend the C6 light-wall rule to: "darker than the wood front by at least 1.4:1, at least 4.5:1 against the wall, and luminance at least 0.012 (never black)". Birch `#4a4337` still passes. The dark-wall rule is unchanged.
  - Display keeps its float. A `#2e1c12` plinth still reads dark under a walnut carcass.
- **Severity:** should.

### K5. In the evening, the skirting wash re-creates the rail between the legs
- **Concerns:** L4.2 and L6.2 in the evening, `pair-grid-35-lit.png` (oak and mahogany rows: the "pale stubs" the round leaves open), C12.
- **What I see:** `look-L4-evening-desktop.jpg` against `look-L4-desktop.jpg`, at the base of section 1. In the evening the skirting band behind the legs turns into a lighter grey bar. - **Why:** C12 quietened the skirting so it would stop reading as a stretcher rail. This puts the rail back at night. It also breaks the lighting model. The strip lights sit inside the cubbies, so they cannot reach the wall behind the piece. The area under and behind a piece on legs should be a little darker in the evening, not lighter.
- **Change:** Drop `.section-light::after` and keep only the floor glow in front of the base (`::before`). That is 11 fewer pseudo-elements on the 400 sample. If the owner likes some glow near the floor, start the wash at the feet line, so it lies on the floor and not on the skirting.
- **Severity:** should.

### K6. The cost table understates how much more Deep paints
- **Concerns:** S-2, the cost table in section 2, `measurements/measurements-cost.json`, C13.
- **What I see:** `perf2.mjs` counts `background-image` on every element, including the lamp's pools and the `section-light` pseudo-elements, which are under `display: none` when the lights are Off. I counted only what is rendered on the same phone page. "Today" paints **2,577** layers, not 2,929 (352 hidden lamp layers were counted). So Deep merged with Always on is **+58 %** (4,084), not +39 %. Six layers is **+71 %** (4,414), not +51 %. The trace ratios are the honest ones: merged is +33 % raster and +46 % paint, and six layers is +41 % raster and +39 % paint. Merged painted *slower* than six layers on Classic (190.5 against 181.7 ms), which is headless noise.
- **Why:** The owner decides S-2 partly on cost. The direction does not change (merged is cheaper), but the size of the jump does.
- **Change:** Correct the percentages in section 2 and in the UI-SPEC, and count only rendered layers in the build's cost check. State that the merged gain is fewer layers and no clip-path, not a measured paint win. Keep "a real phone is still needed" as written.
- **Severity:** should (the numbers the owner reads).

## S-1. Paint the falloff on the page: **yes**

- **Phone cost:** It removes a fixed, full-viewport composited layer. At 390 × 844 CSS px and 3× density that is about 12 MB of GPU texture, composited on every scroll frame. On the page, the gradient rasterises once into the root's tiles at the top of the document, at almost no cost.
- **Desktop:** It brings back sharp text, the F1 grey anti-aliasing issue, and drops the warm-brown special case. Phones use greyscale anti-aliasing anyway, so there the win is the layer.
- **What the visitor loses:** Very little. On oak and mahogany the falloff is 0.06 and hard to see either way (`round2-falloff.png`). On stone and birch the glow stays at the top, like a ceiling light.
- **Build notes:** Put it on `body` with the wall colour, so it propagates to the canvas, and leave `html` without a background. Use `no-repeat`, `top`, `100% 100lvh`. Never use `background-attachment: fixed`: that recreates the fixed layer, and mobile browsers ignore it or handle it badly. Update the E6 line and the overflow test that mention the fixed layer. The preview wrapper paints the same gradient on itself.

## S-2. Deep construction: **merged (four layers on one element)**

- **Look:** It reads as a box seen from slightly above: a thin dark ceiling, walls darkening toward the back, a lighter floor. The mitred version reads more like a frame. The one loss is a small square notch where each side wall stands on the floor plate (`round2-depth.png`, merged, bottom corners). A true view from above would show a diagonal there. It is hard to see at desktop size and invisible on a phone.
- **Phone cost:** It stays within the contract's five-layer cap. There is no clip-path on 165 elements, 330 fewer rendered gradient layers on the 400 phone page (about 7.5 % of what Deep paints), and about 6 % less raster in the trace. Be honest about the total, though (K6): Deep with lights on still paints about +58 % gradient layers and +33 % raster against today. Keep Deep a choice, never a default, and keep the real-phone check open.
- **Build note:** Try hosting the four layers on the cubby itself and dropping `cubby-deco` (K10).
