# Plan 07-35: Tracer - on a desktop the filters fold into a rail at the left and open into a panel beside the cabinet

## Phase Goal

ROADMAP goal (not yet in user-story form; flagged, see `/gsd mvp-phase 7`): "A visitor can narrow the cabinet to tonight's game by dimming everything else, and can switch between one big cabinet and one cabinet per storage location."

Coverage note: this plan starts the owner-approved rework in `07-UI-SPEC.md`, "Amendment: the filter rail and panel (desktop)" (AM-0 to AM-16), decided in `07-SIDEBAR-PROPOSAL.md`, "Owner's answer". It lifts AM-0, AM-2 (states and the attribute), AM-3 (page frame, nesting, no top bar), AM-4 and AM-5 (markup and look), AM-6 (push look and the fly-over's styling), AM-12 (icons). Later plans add the fly-over's behaviour (07-36), the rail state and the phone button (07-37), the geometry and columns (07-38), the remembered state (07-39), the flows (07-40), the migrated tests (07-41, 07-42) and the contrast and access checks (07-43).

<objective>
Wire one end-to-end path of the new desktop filters: the page loads with a folded rail at the left edge and nothing at the top; the rail's three-line button opens the panel beside the cabinet with the existing control set moved into it; a typed title dims the rest; the head button folds it again. Then finish the dock's look in one stylesheet block and take the old two-row desktop bar out, leaving the phone's slim bar and sheet exactly as they are.

Purpose: prove the dock's architecture (one dock element, two children, one control set moved by the width handler, state on the root element) on the real page before anything is built on it.
Output: the dock markup, `filter-dock.js`, the dock block of `filters.css`, the moved units, test helpers and `FilterDockGeometryTests`.
</objective>
