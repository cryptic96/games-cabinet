# Plan 08-15: The container caps set, and a controlled flood slows the cabinet without starving its neighbours

## Phase Goal

ROADMAP goal (not yet in user-story form; flagged, see `/gsd mvp-phase 8`): "The site is reachable from the internet over HTTPS through the existing reverse proxy, resilient to traffic floods and unable to starve the other guests on the host, and the owner opens it only after a go-public checklist passes."

Coverage note: the container half of D-07, the flood half of D-08 and the pass bar of D-09 on the measurement build; lifts SEC-04 concurrency and three prohibitions. The same flood runs again on `v1.0.0` in plan 08-20 as the verification.

<objective>
Have the owner set the container's caps from the proposal, verify them from inside, then run the controlled flood twice from the proxy container with the proposed unit limits applied at run time, measuring the D-09 pass bar and the recovery, and confirming the proxy counts per address.

Purpose: SEC-06's "a flood slows the app without starving the other guests" is measured, and the caps are confirmed or corrected before they go into the repository.
Output: the "Flood" section of `08-MEASUREMENTS.md`, the flood windows for the owner, the tool removed.
</objective>

Research: "Supporting" tools table (`hey` from the Ubuntu archive, 6.9 MB, libc only; the proxy has `python3`, `ss`, `awk`, no `curl`), "Flood commands", "Pitfall 6" (a flood is a TLS flood first; the proxy has one core and 512 MB and five other sites; its access log has no rotation), "Pattern 7" (in-flight counts streams for their lifetime; assumptions A2 and A3), "Pattern 4" (the server-wide connection ceiling and the ops health check), assumption A8 (`hey` flags from memory: run `hey -h` first).

<interfaces>
- `ssh traefik-lxc` and `ssh cabinet-lxc` (sudo). Hostnames and addresses come from the workstation shell (`CABINET_PUBLIC_HOST`, and for the neighbour timing `CABINET_NEIGHBOUR_URL`, a page of another site behind the same proxy, set by Claude in the shell from the proxy's own configuration and never written down); on the proxy the cabinet's address comes from the dynamic file's service URL, read into a shell variable only.
- Name resolution on the proxy is checked, not assumed: the home gateway intercepts DNS and `hey` has no resolve override. Before the first flood, `getent hosts <public host>` on the proxy must return one of the proxy's own addresses (`ip -o addr show`), and `ip route get <that address>` must show local delivery with no `via` hop through the gateway. If not, the name is pinned temporarily to the proxy's own address with one line in the proxy's `/etc/hosts` (outside the repository, covered by the owner's go for tools on the proxy, removed in the clean-up) and the check repeated; the record says only "resolution local: yes/no (pinned: yes/no)". The first flood then exercises TLS, the router, the outer wall and the app.
- The second flood targets `http://<cabinet address>:5080/` directly (the cabinet's firewall admits that port from the proxy only) and must send the public hostname as `Host` (`hey -host <public host>`), because host filtering answers 400 to any other host before an app limit runs; it also sends `X-Forwarded-For: 203.0.113.7`, which the app believes because the proxy is the known proxy.
- The verdict format, checked mechanically: a "Verdict" table whose data lines are exactly `| <flood> | <row> | <value> |`, floods `measure-router` and `measure-direct`, rows `neighbour`, `ssh`, `ops-health`, `host-cpu`, `recovery` (each exactly once per flood), values `PASS` or `FAIL`, and `pending owner` allowed only on the `host-cpu` row.
- Run-time unit limits: `sudo systemctl set-property --runtime cabinet.service MemoryHigh=<v> MemoryMax=<v> CPUQuota=<v>%`; undo with `sudo systemctl revert cabinet.service`.
- Recovery evidence: `systemctl show cabinet -p NRestarts`, `memory.events` `oom_kill`, `journalctl -u cabinet --since <t> | grep -ci 'out of memory'`, health on loopback.
- Live stream held from the proxy with a short `python3` stdlib script: POST `/cabinet/live/negotiate?negotiateVersion=1`, then GET `/cabinet/live?id=<connectionToken>` with `Accept: text/event-stream`, kept open (written to the proxy's `/tmp` and deleted afterwards).
</interfaces>

Repository rules: numbers only in the record; no hostnames, addresses or middleware names; never commit to `main`.
</context>

1. Apply the proposed unit limits at run time on the cabinet. On the proxy: `sudo apt-get update && sudo apt-get install -y hey`, then `hey -h` to confirm the flags (including `-host`). Name-resolution check on the proxy as in the interfaces (`getent hosts`, `ip route get`); pin the name in `/etc/hosts` only if the check fails, then repeat it; if it still fails, stop and report. Record "resolution local: yes/no (pinned: yes/no)" only.
2. Flood one, through the public router with TLS: steps of about 20 seconds at 20, 50, 100 and 200 workers against `https://<public host>/` and then `/cabinet/status`. During each step, in parallel: the workstation samples the neighbour once a second; `ssh cabinet-lxc true` round trips every 5 seconds; inside the cabinet, `/health` on loopback every 2 seconds; the cabinet's `cpu.stat`, `memory.current` and `memory.events` every second; record per step the status split from `hey` (200, 429, 400, other), latencies, the neighbour's times, and the generator's own CPU share on the proxy (`ps -o %cpu= -C hey` once a second, mean and peak per step); flag a step "generator-dominated" in the per-step table when the generator's share exceeds 50 % of the proxy's core in a step where the neighbour's median exceeded 1.5× its baseline (the flag is shown to the owner in plan 08-16; it is a note, not a verdict value). Stop rule: abort the running step immediately when the neighbour's median response time (over its last 5 one-second samples) exceeds 2× its baseline median, then record the abort and step down to the previous step size.
3. Held streams (A2, A3): first, with no streams held, find the `hey` concurrency C0 at which page loads from the proxy start to get in-flight refusals; then hold 20 live streams to the public route with the python script and find C20 the same way. Streams count as in flight for their whole life when C0 - C20 is about 20; record both numbers and the verdict "A2 confirmed: yes/no". While the 20 streams are held, a `hey` burst below C20 must get 200s (429s only from the rate limit). Then saturate the proxy address's in-flight cap and confirm a page load from the workstation (another address) still gets 200 (A3: counted per address, not per host).
4. Flood two, straight at the app port: the same steps against `http://<cabinet address>:5080/cabinet/status` with `-host <public host>` and `-H "X-Forwarded-For: 203.0.113.7"`, then a burst of negotiate POSTs with the same Host and forwarded header (the app's window answers 429), with the same parallel samples and the same per-step status split (200, 429, 400, other).
5. Recovery: one minute after the last step, `NRestarts` and `oom_kill` unchanged, no out-of-memory line in the journal, health `Healthy`, the LAN route home page 200 from the workstation, `build/check-public-routes.sh --from-lan` passes. Record the proxy's access log size and `memory.peak` after.
6. Clean up: `sudo apt-get remove --purge -y hey && sudo apt-get autoremove -y`, remove the python script and any `/etc/hosts` pin on the proxy; `sudo systemctl revert cabinet.service` on the cabinet.
7. Append to "Flood": per step and flood the numbers above, the UTC start and end of each flood window, the held-streams results, the recovery evidence and any cap the numbers say should change (to be settled in plan 08-16). Then the "Verdict" table in the format of the interfaces (floods `measure-router` and `measure-direct`), one value per row. Validity rule: a flood whose steps show any 400 answer, or in which the app answered no 200 or 429, did not reach the app's limits, so every row of that flood is FAIL. Rows: (1) `neighbour`: median within 2× baseline at every step up to the agreed largest step of 200 workers (any abort under the stop rule is FAIL); (2) `ssh`: every round trip completed within 5 seconds; (3) `ops-health`: loopback health answered every probe with 200; (4) `host-cpu`: host CPU peak not saturated (the owner's reading of the Proxmox host graph for the window: written as `pending owner` here and filled in plan 08-16); (5) `recovery`: restart count and `oom_kill` unchanged and no out-of-memory line in the journal. Send the owner the flood windows to read the host CPU graph for (asked in plan 08-16).
