# Plan 08-15: The container caps set, and a controlled flood slows the cabinet without starving its neighbours

## Phase Goal

ROADMAP goal (not yet in user-story form; flagged, see `/gsd mvp-phase 8`): "The site is reachable from the internet over HTTPS through the existing reverse proxy, resilient to traffic floods and unable to starve the other guests on the host, and the owner opens it only after a go-public checklist passes."

Coverage note: the container half of D-07, the flood half of D-08 and the pass bar of D-09 on the measurement build; lifts SEC-04 concurrency and three prohibitions. The same flood runs again on `v1.0.0` in plan 08-20 as the verification.

<objective>
Have the owner set the container's caps from the proposal, verify them from inside, then run the controlled flood twice from the proxy container with the proposed unit limits applied at run time, measuring the D-09 pass bar and the recovery, and confirming the proxy counts per address.

Purpose: SEC-06's "a flood slows the app without starving the other guests" is measured, and the caps are confirmed or corrected before they go into the repository.
Output: the "Flood" section of `08-MEASUREMENTS.md`, the flood windows for the owner, the tool removed.
</objective>

<interfaces>
- `ssh traefik-lxc` and `ssh cabinet-lxc` (sudo). Hostnames and addresses come from the workstation shell (`CABINET_PUBLIC_HOST`, and for the neighbour timing `CABINET_NEIGHBOUR_URL`, a page of another site behind the same proxy, set by Claude in the shell from the proxy's own configuration and never written down); on the proxy the cabinet's address comes from the dynamic file's service URL, read into a shell variable only.
- From the proxy, the public hostname resolves to the proxy itself through local DNS, so the first flood exercises TLS, the router, the outer wall and the app; the second targets `http://<cabinet address>:5080/` directly (the cabinet's firewall admits that port from the proxy only), sending `X-Forwarded-For: 203.0.113.7`, which the app believes because the proxy is the known proxy.
- Run-time unit limits: `sudo systemctl set-property --runtime cabinet.service MemoryHigh=<v> MemoryMax=<v> CPUQuota=<v>%`; undo with `sudo systemctl revert cabinet.service`.
- Recovery evidence: `systemctl show cabinet -p NRestarts`, `memory.events` `oom_kill`, `journalctl -u cabinet --since <t> | grep -ci 'out of memory'`, health on loopback.
- Live stream held from the proxy with a short `python3` stdlib script: POST `/cabinet/live/negotiate?negotiateVersion=1`, then GET `/cabinet/live?id=<connectionToken>` with `Accept: text/event-stream`, kept open (written to the proxy's `/tmp` and deleted afterwards).
</interfaces>

Repository rules: numbers only in the record; no hostnames, addresses or middleware names; never commit to `main`.
</context>

  <action>
1. Apply the proposed unit limits at run time on the cabinet. On the proxy: `sudo apt-get update && sudo apt-get install -y hey`, then `hey -h` to confirm the flags.
2. Flood one, through the public router with TLS: steps of about 20 seconds at 20, 50, 100 and 200 workers against `https://<public host>/` and then `/cabinet/status`. During each step, in parallel: the workstation samples the neighbour once a second; `ssh cabinet-lxc true` round trips every 5 seconds; inside the cabinet, `/health` on loopback every 2 seconds; the cabinet's `cpu.stat`, `memory.current` and `memory.events` every second; record per step the 200/429 split from `hey`, latencies, the neighbour's times. Stop rule: abort the running step immediately when the neighbour's median response time (over its last 5 one-second samples) exceeds 2× its baseline median, then record the abort and step down to the previous step size.
3. Held streams (A2, A3): from the proxy hold 20 live streams to the public route with the python script; while they are held, a `hey` burst of page loads from the proxy must get 200s (and 429s only from the rate limit, not from in-flight); then saturate the proxy address's in-flight cap and confirm a page load from the workstation (another address) still gets 200.
4. Flood two, straight at the app port: the same steps against `http://<cabinet address>:5080/cabinet/status` with `-H "X-Forwarded-For: 203.0.113.7"`, then a burst of negotiate POSTs with the same header (the app's window answers 429), with the same parallel samples.
5. Recovery: one minute after the last step, `NRestarts` and `oom_kill` unchanged, no out-of-memory line in the journal, health `Healthy`, the LAN route home page 200 from the workstation, `build/check-public-routes.sh --from-lan` passes. Record the proxy's access log size and `memory.peak` after.
6. Clean up: `sudo apt-get remove --purge -y hey && sudo apt-get autoremove -y` and remove the python script on the proxy; `sudo systemctl revert cabinet.service` on the cabinet.
7. Append to "Flood": per step and flood the numbers above, the UTC start and end of each flood window, the held-streams results, the recovery evidence and any cap the numbers say should change (to be settled in plan 08-16). Then a "Verdict" table per flood with one PASS or FAIL per row: (1) neighbour median within 2× baseline at every step up to the agreed largest step of 200 workers (any abort under the stop rule is FAIL); (2) SSH responsive (every round trip completed within 5 seconds); (3) loopback ops health answered every probe with 200; (4) host CPU peak not saturated (the owner's reading of the Proxmox host graph for the window: written as "pending owner" here and filled in plan 08-16); (5) restart count and `oom_kill` unchanged and no out-of-memory line in the journal. Send the owner the flood windows to read the host CPU graph for (asked in plan 08-16).
  </action>
