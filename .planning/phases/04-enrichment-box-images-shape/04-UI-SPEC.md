# Phase 4 — UI Design Contract

> Visual and interaction contract for real box art, art-coloured spines, true-proportion boxes, thin-box text, expansion labels, plinth and cabinet polish, phone density and the review artefacts. Generated by gsd-ui-researcher, verified by gsd-ui-checker.
>
> **Reading guide.** This phase **extends** the approved layout contract (`02-UI-SPEC.md`) and the sync contract (`03-UI-SPEC.md`). Where this file is silent, those two files apply. Where this file contradicts one of them, **this file wins for this phase** and says so in "What this phase changes". ## Spacing Scale

Unchanged from the layout contract (multiples of 4: 4, 8, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64). Page chrome gains no new element in this phase, so there are no new spacing usages and **no new exceptions**.

In-cabinet geometry stays in millimetres scaled by `--u`. The following geometry rules are new:

- A box is drawn at `max(real thickness, one-line floor)` (see "Thin boxes"). Real sizes, a flat cover's shape or the estimated size set every box's width and height; the renderer still draws exactly the rectangle the layout gives it.
- The fill bars around fitted art are not a spacing token: they are whatever space the fit leaves inside the box.

## Color

### Chrome (60 / 30 / 10)

Exactly the layout contract. Dominant `#3b2416` backdrop, secondary `#6b4226` furniture, interior `--ink`, edge highlight `--wood-edge`, wall text `--wall-text`, accent `#d9b98a`.

**Accent reserved for (exhaustive, FIXED, unchanged):**

1. The background of the current sample link (local development only)
2. The "+N more" marker chip
3. The keyboard focus ring (2px solid, 2px offset) on every focusable element
4. The "Try again" button background in the cabinet load-error state

Art-derived colours never use accent, never appear on the frame, headings or footer, and are not part of the 60/30/10 split (neither is the placeholder palette). Destructive colour: not applicable, no destructive action in this phase.

### Colours that come from box art

Computed **once per image during sync**, stored with the game, never computed in the browser (the snapshot carries them; the layout JSON passes them through).

| Colour | What it is | Used for |
|--------|------------|----------|
| Spine colour | The art's main colour with plain backdrops ignored, lightness nudged only when the title needs it (rules below) | `--bg` of spines, upright expansions, flat boxes, expansion layers and orphan boxes; also `--bg` of the generated-cover fallback for that game |
| Text colour | `#ffffff` or `#000000`, whichever passes (below) | `--fg` on those boxes |
| Four edge colours | The mean colour of the outermost 2 percent of each side of the picture (top, right, bottom, left; **STARTING VALUE** 2 percent, at least 2 px) | The fill bars of an art cover, one colour per side |

**Why edge colours and not the spine colour for the fill (decided from the reference render):** a three-dimensional shot on a white backdrop, fitted into a taller box, looks like a clean photo card when the bars are the backdrop's own colour, and like a banded picture when the bars take the box's red (`ui-refs/art-fit-and-thin-spines.png`, D against E). Edge colours also make a cover with a dark footer band or a light border continue seamlessly into its bars. The fill is still "a colour taken from the art", as required.

### Spine colour rules (FIXED unless tagged)

1. **True to the art.** The extracted colour is used as it is. Only lightness may move, and only when the title cannot reach contrast without it. No saturation boost, no hue shift, no palette snapping, no minimum or maximum lightness otherwise.
2. **Plain backdrops never win.** A backdrop is a region that touches the picture's edge and is uniform within a small colour distance (white, near-white, grey, black, a flat colour field around a product shot). Pixels in it are excluded when picking the colour. A red box on white gives red; a cover with a thick white frame gives the art inside the frame. **Exception that must not fail:** a picture that is plain edge to edge, or whose art is all backdrop colour (an all-white cover), has nothing left after exclusion. It uses the picture's overall mean colour, so a white cover gets a white or cream spine, never an error and never the palette.
3. **Text is white or black, whichever reaches at least 4.5:1** under the strongest furniture shade (`SpinePalette.MaxShadePercent`, 20 percent of `#140a04`). The title sits under a shade that is 0.20 at the top of the cubby and fades to 0, so the check is made **at both ends**: the pair must pass with no shade and with the full 20 percent shade, blended as browsers blend (gamma-encoded sRGB channels). White text is weakest with no shade, black text is weakest under full shade.
4. **Nudge rule.** When neither text colour passes at both ends, move the colour's OKLCH lightness by the smallest step (0.005 granularity) that makes one of them pass, up or down, whichever is the smaller move; keep hue and chroma and, if the result leaves the sRGB gamut, reduce chroma just enough to stay inside. Measured over a 17-step grid of all sRGB colours (4,913 colours): with white and black text, **77 percent of colours need no nudge, and the largest nudge any colour needs is 0.065 OKLCH lightness** (mean 0.0076). With white and `--ink` (`#2a1a10`) as the pair, 68 percent need none and the largest is 0.090, which is why art spines use pure black rather than `--ink`. A nudge of that size is visible only as a slightly lighter or darker shade of the same colour.
5. **A unit test** (property style) runs the extractor's contrast function over that 17-step grid and asserts the result passes at both ends for every colour. A second test asserts that any stored pair that fails the check, or is not a six-digit hexadecimal colour, is **ignored on read** and the game falls back to its palette tone. The existing palette test (every tone passes at the cap) stays.
6. **Very dark art stays dark.** A black box gets a black or near-black spine with white text. It must stay distinguishable from the cubby back through the existing 1px light inset edge and contact shadow, which spines keep. If review shows black spines vanish, the lever is a lightness floor (tuning register), not a change to the rule above.
7. **Fallback chain per game:** art spine colour, else palette tone (`SpinePalette.ToneFor`). A game with no art keeps the palette tone it has today.

### Placeholder palette (unchanged)

The 12 tones and their pairs stay as the fallback. FIXED: 12 or more entries, each at or above 4.5:1 at the shade cap.

## Box visual contract

### What each placement kind shows

| Kind | Shows | Picture? |
|------|-------|----------|
| Cover with usable art | The art, fitted whole, on the four edge colours | **Yes. The only kind that loads an image** |
| Cover without usable art (none, failed download, undecodable) | The generated cover: palette tone, pattern, title plate | No |
| Spine, upright expansion | One solid art colour, title on it, the two thin rules near the ends | No |
| Flat box, expansion layer, orphan expansion | One solid art colour, title on it | No |
| "+N more" marker | Accent chip, unchanged | No |

Because only face-out covers use art, at most about the cover share of the collection loads an image, and spines, flat boxes and layers cost no request.

### Art cover (D-06: never crop, never overlay)

- **Markup.** The cover button holds one `<img class="cover-art">` and nothing else: no title plate, no pattern, no label span. The button carries `data-art="true"` and `data-fit="width"`, `"height"` or `"exact"` (see below). `aria-label` and `title` keep the full title as today.
- **Fit.** `position: absolute; inset: 0; width: 100%; height: 100%; object-fit: contain; object-position: center; display: block; pointer-events: none; user-select: none`, with `draggable = false`. The picture is scaled down to fit entirely inside the box front, centred. It is never cropped, stretched, rotated, recoloured, blurred, masked or given rounded corners.
- **Fill.** The button's own background fills what the picture leaves:
  - `data-fit="width"` (the picture is relatively wider than the box, so it fills the width and bars sit above and below): `linear-gradient(to bottom, var(--edge-t) 50%, var(--edge-b) 50%)`
  - `data-fit="height"` (the picture is relatively narrower, so bars sit left and right): `linear-gradient(to right, var(--edge-l) 50%, var(--edge-r) 50%)`
  - `data-fit="exact"` (the shapes agree to within a pixel): `var(--edge-t)`; no bars appear
  - The server states the fit in the layout data; the renderer does not compare shapes.
- **How big are the bars.** For a flat cover, the box shape comes from real dimensions that agree with the cover within the disagreement margin (starting value 12 percent of the aspect ratio, tuned in review; if they disagree by more, the cover's shape is used and the bars vanish). At the starting margin the bars total at most about 11 percent of the box width, so about 5 percent per side. A three-dimensional shot shown face-out keeps the box's own true shape, so its bars can be large (a square shot in a 3-by-4 box leaves bars of about 12 percent of the height above and below); those bars are the shot's own backdrop colour and read as a photo card. **That is the accepted look of "3D shot anyway".**
- **What is left of the old look.** On an art cover the 1px light inset edge is not drawn (it would sit under the picture anyway, and nothing may be drawn over the art). The outer contact shadow stays.
- **The one layer above the art is the furniture's shade** (`.cubby::after`, alpha at most 0.20, fading out by 20 percent of the cubby height). It is the cabinet's own lighting falling on every box alike, not a modification of the picture, and it is not new. It is the only overlay and must stay the only one. **Flagged in "Conflicts and open points" for the owner to confirm.**
- **Hover and focus** are unchanged (6 mm lift under `(hover: hover)`, 2px accent focus ring at z-index 2). Reduced motion removes the lift as today.
- **No text on art covers.** An expansion that faces out in a small collection loses its visible "Expansion" line on an art cover (the art cannot carry it); its accessible name and tooltip still say "{title}, expansion". Flagged below.

### Generated cover fallback (unchanged look, one fix)

- Appearance is exactly today's: palette (or art) background, one of six patterns from the game's hash, a solid title plate about 40 percent of the cover height, up to 4 lines.
- **Fix for the cropped fourth line.** The plate shows as many **whole** lines as it can hold and the last line shown carries the ellipsis; no line is ever cut through the middle. Line count steps down with the cover's rendered height (**STARTING VALUE**, at the 12px floor with line height 1.2 and 3px plate padding): 4 lines from 64px, 3 lines from 50px, 2 lines from 36px, 1 line below. Implement with `@container` rules on the cover (`container-type: size` on cover buttons) setting `-webkit-line-clamp`, or an equivalent CSS-only rule. Larger font sizes only occur on wider and therefore taller covers, so the 12px thresholds hold.
- Test: in a browser test, for every generated cover on the 65 and 400 samples at 390 px and 1440 px, the label's bottom edge lies inside the plate and the plate lies inside the cover.
- It stands in when: the game has no usable image, the download failed, the image was undecodable, or the browser raises an `error` event on the `<img>`.
- **On a browser-side failure** the renderer replaces the `<img>` and the fill with the generated cover's markup for that button (`.cover-plate` and the pattern), keeping `--bg` and `--fg`. Nothing visible announces the failure, no copy, no icon.

### Boxes coloured from art (spine, upright expansion, flat box, layer, orphan)

- Same structure as today: `--bg` and `--fg` set per element, the two thin rules on spines and upright expansions in `color-mix(in srgb, var(--fg) 35%, transparent)`, the 1px light inset edge and contact shadow. Nothing about the structure changes; only the two colours do.
- Spine, flat box and layer of one game are the same colour as that game's fallback cover, because they come from one stored colour. A game standing as a spine and the same game lying flat are never different colours.
- Colours stay recognisable rather than toned down: a red box has a red spine.

### Thin boxes: true thickness down to a one-line minimum (D-09)

Derivation (the numbers are not copied blindly; the engine derives them as it does today, from `ReadabilityFloor.Millimetres(targetPx, smallestRenderedWidthPx, renderedWidthMm)`):

- One line of 12px text at line height 1.2 needs 14.4px, so the one-line target is **15px** (`OneLineLabelPx`). Two lines need 29px as before (`TwoLineSpinePx`) and, for a horizontal box, 36px (`TwoLineLabelPx`).
- Desktop smallest rendering: 592px for 1,304 mm. Phone smallest rendering: 304px for 744 mm (320px screen, 8px gutters).

| Floor | Target | Desktop | Phone | Replaces |
|-------|--------|---------|-------|----------|
| Any box, one line (spine width, flat box and orphan height, upright expansion width) | `max(15px one line, tap floor)`; the tap floor is 24px on phone and does not apply on desktop | **34 mm** (about 15 to 16px) | **59 mm** (24px) | desktop already 34; phone already 59 |
| Upright expansion width, two lines | 29px | 64 mm | 71 mm | was the minimum; now the threshold for showing a second line |
| Orphan height, two lines | 36px | 80 mm | 89 mm | was the minimum; now the threshold for showing a second line |
| Expansion layer height | one-line floor, at most 70 mm | **34 mm** (was 40) | 59 mm (unchanged; the tap floor already governed) | desktop 40 mm minimum layer |

**The rule (FIXED).** A box is drawn at `max(its real thickness, the one-line floor of the profile)`. The second line of an upright expansion or an orphan box ("Expansion for {base}", or the interim "Expansion") is drawn **only when the drawn thickness is at least the two-line threshold above**; below it the box shows its title alone, ellipsised when needed, in the plain spine or flat-box title style. The layout data says which (`showBaseLine`), computed by the engine from millimetres, so it is deterministic and never depends on the visitor's screen. The accessible name and tooltip always carry the full text ("{title}, expansion for {base}").

**What this honestly changes on phone.** The phone one-line floor (59 mm) is the 24px tap target, which the owner's rule forbids going below. So **phone base-game spines do not get thinner**: a real 40 mm box still reads 59 mm. What phone gains is that upright expansions drop from 71 to 59 mm and orphans from 89 to 59 mm. Desktop gains the large drop (64 to 34 mm, 80 to 34 mm). See "Conflicts and open points" for the lever if the owner wants phone spines thinner.

Folded polish resolved here: the two truncated lines on short upright expansions (the second line is dropped, the title gets the whole height) and the 50 to 63 mm expansions drawn at 64 or 71 mm (they now draw at 50 to 63 mm on desktop, and 59 mm on phone).

The thick-expansion threshold (50 mm deep stands upright) is unchanged as a **STARTING VALUE** and is revisited in review: if 50 to 59 mm uprights with a lone title look skinny beside their base, raise it.

Desktop at 34 mm is tight: a 12px bold line fits with almost no air (see the narrow spines in `ui-refs/art-fit-and-thin-spines.png`). It is legible, and it is the first lever to pull if the owner finds it cramped (raise to 38 mm, tuning register).

### Expansion labels

| Situation | Visible text | Accessible name and tooltip |
|-----------|--------------|------------------------------|
| Orphan expansion, base title known, room for two lines | title, then `Expansion for {base}` | `{title}, expansion for {base}` |
| Orphan expansion, base known, room for one line | title only | `{title}, expansion for {base}` |

| Expansion shown as an art cover (small collections) | none (art only) | `{title}, expansion for {base}` or `{title}, expansion` |

Copy strings are unchanged; only which rows show their visible line is new.

### "+N more" marker (accessibility fix only)

Look is unchanged: accent chip, `--ink` text, weight 600, 12px, about 40 mm tall (**FIXED** accent use). The text stays `+{N} more`. The accessible name now **contains the visible text, at its start**, for label-in-name and speech input:

- `+{N} more expansions for {base title}`; for one: `+1 more expansion for {base title}`.

### Image loading behaviour

- **No layout shift.** The button already has its millimetre size. The `<img>` is absolutely positioned inside it and also carries `width` and `height` properties set from the stored pixel dimensions of the served variant, so nothing depends on the file arriving.
- **Attributes.** `alt=""` (the button's `aria-label` names the box, the same pattern the layout contract used for decorative content), `loading="lazy"`, `decoding="async"`, `draggable=false`. No `srcset` is required: the layout JSON is already per profile, so the server hands each cover one URL (see variant rule).
- **While loading.** The box shows its edge-colour fill and nothing else. No spinner, no skeleton, no shimmer, no fade-in: the picture appears when decoded. (The motion baseline is still none.)
- **Variant rule (visual floor; the plan sets the exact widths).** Two variants per image, nominally 240 and 480 px wide, WebP, served from the site's own origin with a content hash in the name. At 390px and 1440px viewports a cover's image must be at least 1.5 times its CSS width, and no cover loads more than 480 px wide. The review reports the total image bytes for the 65 and 400 samples.
- **Spines, flat boxes, layers and orphans never load an image.**
- **Off-screen sections** keep `content-visibility: auto` and lazy images, so a 400-item cabinet does not fetch or decode art for sections nobody scrolled to.
- **Failure** (HTTP error, undecodable file): the generated cover, silently.
- **Stored image files are served immutable** (a content hash in the name); a changed image changes the URL, so the browser never shows stale art.

## Plinth arch and general cabinet polish

Cue list the polish answers (from the render of the current build, `ui-refs/plinth-current.png`):

1. The arch under the plinth is a feathered smudge: no edge, no depth, so it reads as a shadow, not as an opening in the wood.
2. With no readable opening, the feet are not readable either, so the cabinet looks as if it sits flat on its plinth.
3. Everything else in the finish (lit edges, grain, planked back, moulding, shade) already reads as furniture and **stays as it is**.

**Plinth arch (default: the apron).** Replace the `.section-base::before` gradient with a shaped opening drawn with `border-radius`, so it has a real edge. Recipe, **STARTING VALUES**:

```css
.cabinet {
  --arch-rise: 40;
  --arch-radius: 110;
  --arch-alpha-top: 0.92;
  --arch-alpha-bottom: 0.72;
  --arch-lit: 0.14;
}

.section-base::before {
  border-radius:
    calc(var(--arch-radius) * var(--u)) calc(var(--arch-radius) * var(--u)) 0 0 / 100% 100% 0 0;
  background: linear-gradient(
    to bottom,
    rgb(8 4 2 / var(--arch-alpha-top)),
    rgb(30 16 8 / var(--arch-alpha-bottom))
  );
  box-shadow:
    inset 0 calc(6 * var(--u)) calc(7 * var(--u)) rgb(0 0 0 / 0.45),
    calc(-1 * var(--lw)) calc(-1 * var(--lw)) 0 rgb(255 240 220 / var(--arch-lit)),
    var(--lw) 0 0 rgb(0 0 0 / 0.40);
}
```

- Keeps the existing position (`left` and `right` at `--foot-width`, `bottom: 0`, `height: --arch-rise`), the plinth's own tone and grain, and the floor shadow underneath unchanged.
- Reads as: a defined opening with rounded corners where it meets the feet, deepest shade at the top (the plinth board above casts into it), a little of the backdrop colour showing at the bottom, a lit lip on the upper-left curve and a dark one on the lower-right, matching the one light direction.
- Still satisfies the layout contract's rule that the opening is **never a flat backdrop-coloured hole or a hard black cut**: the top is `rgb(8 4 2 / 0.92)` over wood, not black, and the bottom lets the backdrop through.
- Stays inside the section box (the plinth is 80 mm; the arch rises 40 mm and leaves 40 mm of board above it), inside the 6-layer limit, and adds no `filter`.
- `--arch-shade-alpha` is retired; its tests and mentions go with it.
- **Taste call for the review (not a defect):** the **ellipse alternative** (`border-radius: 50% 50% 0 0 / 100% 100% 0 0`, `--arch-rise` 36, same colours) reads as a true shallow arch rather than a shaped apron. Both renders are in `ui-refs/` (`plinth-apron-default.png`, `plinth-ellipse-alternative.png`). Default is the apron because it stays legible at phone width, where a 36 mm lens is only about 14px tall.

**Other polish (all STARTING VALUES, only if the review asks):**

- A soft cast shadow to the right of each box on the cubby back (`2px 0 3px -1px rgb(0 0 0 / 0.35)` as a third box shadow). Off by default because every box already carries two shadows and the performance budget allows at most two per repeated item.
- Foot arrises: a 1px lit edge on each foot's outer left edge. Off by default.

Nothing else about the furniture changes. The shade cap (0.20), the single light direction, the four browns, the 32 mm side allowance and the rule that every painted pixel stays inside the section box are all unchanged.

## Phone cabinet density

**Problem (confirmed in a render of the invented 65-item sample at 390px):** empty rows sit in the middle of earlier sections, not only at their ends, and a sparse section is followed by a new one. Only the last section is trimmed. The 400-item sample takes 12 phone sections.

**Likely cause to confirm, not assume.** The phone design's rows were tuned against invented sizes. The default real-size standard box is 225 x 300 x 60 mm and `MaxSpineHeightMm` is 340 mm, while the phone rows include heights of 260 and 300 mm. Boxes about 300 mm tall do not stand in a 260 mm row. Once real sizes land, the packing changes anyway, so **measure first.**

**Approach, in order (the planner takes the first that meets the acceptance below):**

1. **Retune the section designs to real box sizes (data only).** Adjust phone row heights and cubby widths so most rows take a standard standing box (about 300 mm plus air) and at most one row per section is short. Bump the layout version and re-record the golden files with synthetic samples. No engine rule changes, no new stability exception.
2. **If rows are still left empty: trim empty trailing rows of earlier phone sections** (draw only down to the last row holding a placement, at least 2 rows). This is a drawing-only change but it **is** a new stability exception: placing a later game into a trimmed row makes that section taller again and moves the sections after it. Document it beside the existing exceptions, test it as its own case, and apply it to the phone profile only (desktop sections sit side by side and are aligned at the top).
3. **Not now:** per-location cabinets (the filters phase) will shorten cabinets further.

**Acceptance (STARTING VALUES, measured on the synthetic 400-item sample with a realistic size spread and on the 65-item sample, phone profile, before and after):** no non-last phone section shows more than one row without a placement, and the number of phone sections for 400 items drops from 12. The before and after screenshots at 390px go in the review round. The owner's own judgement on the real collection on a phone is the gate.

### Cover-share screenshots (default share pick)

The owner sees the same real cabinet at three shares and picks one. The pick becomes the committed default of the `CoverSharePercent` setting.

- Candidates: **20, 25 and 33 percent** (about 1 in 5, 1 in 4, 1 in 3). Only the share differs between shots; the collection, sizes, art and every other setting are the same.
- Each candidate at **1440px desktop, full cabinet**, and at **390px phone, the first three screens** (cabinet from the top, with the sections' furniture visible).
- File names and the message carry the share and the actual face-out share after big boxes lie flat (for example "about 1 in 4, actually 21 percent facing out"). Nothing is written onto the page itself.
- Sent as images, never committed.

### Fixtures the review needs (synthetic, generated in code, served by the fake image host)

Local review rounds and the browser tests need these invented pictures, so every rule above can be seen before the real run: a flat cover that matches its box shape; a flat cover relatively wider and one relatively narrower than its box; a three-dimensional shot on white; one on a light grey gradient; one on black; a flat cover with a thick white frame; an all-white cover; a near-black cover; a mid-green cover near the worst contrast case (about `rgb(51 153 51)`); a very wide banner; and a file that cannot be decoded. The mix shown in each review screenshot must include at least one of each placement kind on art colours, one generated-cover fallback, one failed image, a thin upright expansion without its second line, an orphan with two lines and one with one, a "+N more" marker, and the plinth at desktop and phone.

### Review flow

Local rounds against the fake BGG with synthetic pictures (screenshots at 1440px and 390px, plus crops of the arch, a fitted cover with bars, the thinnest spines and one fallback cover); then the server round with the review sheet and the three cover-share sets; then the release the owner approves and checks deployed on desktop and phone. ## Interaction, Motion and Accessibility Baseline (additions)

- **Motion:** none added. No fade-in for pictures, no placeholder animation, no transition on the fallback swap. The only motion in the app is still the 120ms hover lift, removed under reduced motion.
- **Keyboard and focus:** unchanged. One tab stop per box still (the one-tab-stop cabinet is the detail phase's work). The focus ring paints above the art and the shade (z-index 2) and is never clipped.
- **Screen readers:** an art cover is a button named by its title; the `<img>` is `alt=""`. The fallback swap announces nothing. The "+N more" name now contains its visible text. The decorative furniture elements stay `aria-hidden`.
- **Contrast (FIXED):** every title on an art-derived box reaches 4.5:1 at both ends of the furniture shade (see Color). Art covers carry no text, so no contrast requirement applies to the picture; the focus ring against the art is the accent on whatever lies behind it and the 2px offset keeps it clear of the art edge.
- **Colour is never the only carrier of meaning:** an expansion's nature is carried by its name and, where there is room, its second line, never by colour.
- **Reduced data and memory:** lazy images, two small variants, no hover-prefetch, no image for any box that is not a face-out cover.
- **Strict CSP (FIXED):** the page must stay free of violations with images on the same origin. The integration test still fails on `style=` attributes and inline script bodies. A browser test fails on any request to a host other than the site's own.
- **BGG text:** `textContent` only, everywhere.

## Conflicts and open points

These are the places where the decisions and the code pull against each other. The contract states what it assumes; the owner or the planner may overrule.

1. **Phone spines cannot get thinner under the 24px tap rule.** The decision says boxes keep real thickness down to the width a line needs "and never below the 24 px tap target". At the narrowest phone, 24px is 59 mm, which is exactly today's phone spine minimum. So the change only helps phone upright expansions and orphans, and desktop. **Confirmed by the owner (2026-10-07):** the 24px tap floor stays FIXED on every phone. **Lever (declined for now):** derive the phone floors from a 360px screen instead of 320px (about 52 mm), which would put a 320px phone's thinnest spines at about 21px. That breaks the 24px rule on the smallest phones only.
2. **Orphan expansions can lose their visible "Expansion for ..." line.** Applying the one-line floor to orphans means thin orphans show a title only, so a thin orphan looks like a base game until the detail card exists. **Assumed:** accepted, because the decision names that exact line as the one to drop and the accessible name keeps it. The two-line threshold keeps it for orphans at least 80 mm (desktop) or 89 mm (phone) thick.
3. **The furniture shade falls across art.** The decision says nothing is drawn over the art, and the cabinet's shade (alpha at most 0.20, fading in the top 20 percent of a cubby) is the cabinet's lighting, not part of the picture, and falls on every box. **Confirmed by the owner (2026-10-07):** the shade stays on art covers. **Lever (declined for now):** `--shade-alpha` 0 on art covers only, which loses the depth cue on exactly the boxes the owner looks at most.
4. **An expansion shown as an art cover cannot carry the visible "Expansion" tag** the sync contract required for small collections, because no text may sit on the art. Name and tooltip still say it. **Assumed:** accepted; it only occurs in collections under 12 games.
5. **Three-dimensional shots face-out show large bars** (about 12 percent of the box height above and below for a square shot in a 3-by-4 box). They are the shot's own backdrop colour, so they read as a photo card. That is the look the owner chose when preferring "3D shot anyway"; it is a backstop review item.
6. **Stability of box size estimates** is the plan's problem but visible here: a small drift in weight or play time must not move a box. Visual consequence if it does: a cubby rearranges silently after a weekly refresh. Required: coarse classes or rounded inputs, with a test that a small change in each input leaves the layout unchanged.
